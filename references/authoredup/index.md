Three readings that matter for us. The most recommended opener on the platform, the question,
is the weakest of the five. A hook of 40 characters or fewer earns 25% more than one over 200.
And the closing question, the most repeated engagement tip there is, moves the median by 0.07
of a percentage point, which is nothing. What pulls comments is a post worth answering and a
reply from the author in the first hour.

## Viral post examples

Eleven worked examples. The article gives engagement figures for only one of them, so treat the
rest as qualitative.

**1. Hyper-effective LinkedIn post strategy.** Charles Miller. Opens: "I recently discovered a
hyper-effective LinkedIn post strategy". Mechanism: curiosity plus a promise of something the
reader does not have, then the strategy actually explained with examples.
What to take: promise one specific thing and then deliver all of it in the post.

**2. Understanding changes in LinkedIn visibility.** Richard van der Blom. Opens: "Just a quick
shout out to content creators!" Mechanism: names an anxiety the audience already has (falling
impressions), reassures with shared experience, then gives data.
What to take: the "you are not the only one" post. Our version is the manager who thinks their
team is behind on AI and is not.

**3. Revamping your LinkedIn job ads.** Ivana Todorovic. Opens: "We are hiring, Join our team,
Here are our open positions". Mechanism: quotes the phrases everyone recognises back at them,
then says they are losing candidates.
What to take: open by quoting the thing your reader writes every week, then say why it fails.

**4. Debunking SEO stability myths.** Kevin Indig. Opens: "A lot of marketers assume SEO ranks
are stable". 15 shares. Mechanism: names a belief, contradicts it, gives a relatable example and
a fix.
What to take: the correction post. "A lot of owners assume their team is behind on AI."

**5. Challenging conventional LinkedIn networking.** Sandy Zhen. Opens: "80% of LinkedIn is
networking backwards". Mechanism: a bold number, a declared controversy ("People will HATE this
post"), a divisive topic, a personal alternative.
What to take: lead with the number, then your own way of doing it.

**6. Women don't like sales.** Jenny Anderson. Opens: "Women don't like sales. No. You're
wrong." Mechanism: states the wrong belief in someone else's voice, then refutes it with
statistics and structural explanation.
What to take: the two-line contradiction. Wrong belief, then "No."

**7. Clarifying content marketing misconceptions.** Erin Balsa. Opens: "An editorial calendar is
NOT a content strategy". Mechanism: a flat assertive claim, a deep educational explanation,
client-based specifics.
What to take: "A demo is NOT training" is the same shape and it is ours.

**8. A personal journey in marketing.** Nick Bennet. Opens: "When I started off in my career as
a marketer, I was making under 75k". Mechanism: an honest number about himself, ups and downs,
then the strategic changes, ending on encouragement.
What to take: the origin post carries a real number, not adjectives.

**9. Calling out bad LinkedIn practices.** Matt Lakajev. Opens: "I see some stupid shit on
LinkedIn". Mechanism: blunt language, three named behaviours, humour, short punchy lines.
What to take: naming three specific bad practices beats one general complaint. Register is his,
not ours.

**10. The power of repetition in LinkedIn content.** Nick Broekema. Mechanism: contrarian claim
against the originality myth, a television advertising analogy, data, then a step-by-step.
What to take: an analogy from outside the subject does more work than another example inside it.

**11. Exposing LinkedIn growth myths.** Lea Turner. Opens by repeating "NO ONE" is getting the
growth being sold. Mechanism: absolutes with repetition, a reality check, then how the scam
works, then a directive.
What to take: repetition of one phrase is a structure, used once per post.

## Hook examples by type

**Story, the top performer at 2.60%.** Named example: Mischa Collins, "2024: Shock dismissal
from my job. 2026: Official speaker at Cannes Lions. I remember the day I lost my job so
clearly. It was a Friday." 627 reactions, 208 comments. The article's illustrative patterns:
"I got fired on a Tuesday. By Friday I had three offers." / "Three years ago I couldn't get 10
likes. Last month a post hit 2 million views." / "A recruiter ghosted me after the final round.
So I posted the rejection email." / "My biggest client fired us over one sentence in an email."
The move: open mid-action, withhold the resolution, make the reader expand the post.

**Contrarian, 2.31%.** Named example: Charlie Hills, "Stop asking AI to write LinkedIn posts.
Do this instead:" 1,664 reactions, 545 comments. Patterns: "Posting every day is the fastest way
to kill your reach." / "Your engagement isn't low because of the algorithm. It's low because
your first line is boring." / "Most 'personal branding' advice will make you sound like everyone
else."

**Statement, 2.27%, the highest volume.** Named example: Jessie van Breugel, "Claude beats
ChatGPT for getting leads on LinkedIn." 1,934 reactions, over 6,000 comments. Patterns: "I
rewrote my LinkedIn bio 11 times last year. The 11th one finally worked." / "The best post I
ever wrote took four minutes." / "Nobody read my posts for 8 months. Then I changed one thing."

**Results, 2.19%, handle with care.** Named example: Nick Broekema, "My impressions over the
past 7 days went up by 122.9%. Profile views: +99.4%. And the reason why is stupidly simple: I
posted more :)" 104 reactions, 73 comments. The article's note: the numbers grab the eye but the
line that earns the read is "the reason why is stupidly simple". Tired: "How I gained 10,000
followers in 30 days." Better: "I gained 10,000 followers in 30 days and it tanked my inbound
leads. Here's why."

**Question, 2.16%, last.** Named example: Matt Lakajev, "The worst LinkedIn trend right now?
I'll go first: AI comments." 926 reactions, 752 comments. It works because it answers itself
immediately with an opinion. Weak: "Want more engagement on LinkedIn?" Stronger: "Why do your
best posts get 12 likes and your worst ones go viral?"

## The rules, condensed

1. Open with a story or a flat claim. Never a yes or no question.
2. Keep the first line under 40 characters and complete in itself.
3. The hook must sit above the "see more" fold.
4. Make a claim, do not ask permission to be read. A claim opens a gap the reader closes.
5. Quote the phrase your reader writes every week, then say why it fails.
6. Name the belief you are correcting, in their words, before you correct it.
7. A number in the hook needs a twist behind it, or it is scrolled past.
8. Do not bolt a question onto the last line. Reply to comments in the first hour instead.
9. Write three openers per post in three different styles, then pick.
10. Contrarian only where you actually disagree. Forced contrarianism reads as bait.
