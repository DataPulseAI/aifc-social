# Ethan Mollick (Wharton professor, author of Co-Intelligence, writes One Useful Thing), captured 23 Sep 2026
Post archetypes: About one long essay a month on Substack, each built on a named study or a hands-on test of a new model, reposted to LinkedIn as a short summary with the link. Three shapes: the research synthesis ("Making AI Work: Leadership, Lab, and Crowd", 22 May 2025), the field experiment he co-authored (the BCG consultants study, September 2023; the Procter and Gamble study, March 2025), and the periodic practical guide ("An opinionated guide to which AI to use to do stuff", Summer 2026 edition, 23 July 2026).
Hook shapes: A plain observation that sets up a paradox: AI boosts individual work performance, yet the gains are not being captured by companies. Lower-case, modest titles. "Nobody has special information about how to best use AI at your company."
Visual pattern: Almost none. One header image, charts lifted from the papers he cites, screenshots of model output. Credibility comes from the footnotes, not the design.
Funnel mechanic: None in the sales sense. Free Substack with hundreds of thousands of subscribers, books, speaking. Writes every post himself; AI feedback only after a complete draft.
Transfer to AIFC: Take the habit of citing the paper rather than the headline about it, and the finding that individual gains do not become team gains without leadership, a lab and the crowd, which is the argument for a team day rather than seats. He is the tone check against hype. Refuse the length and the academic hedging: state the fact and stop.
