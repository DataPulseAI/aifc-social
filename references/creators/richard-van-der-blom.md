# Richard van der Blom (Just Connecting; the annual paid Algorithm Insights report), captured 23 Sep 2026
The source behind most LinkedIn benchmark figures in circulation, usually quoted without the edition. Read the report once a year and skip the recycled summaries.
Post archetypes: Data drops from his own dataset, one figure plus its implication, and corrections of common LinkedIn advice. 2025 edition (6th): described as 1.8 million posts and 58,000 profiles. 2026 edition (7th): "1.3 million posts" from about 50,000 creators, "200+ pages, 6 chapters, 100+ tactics" (Creator Science podcast, 2 June 2026). Sold, not given away; corporate licensing separately.
Hook shapes: A decline stated flatly ("reach is down 60% for active creators over the last two years"), then what still works.
Visual pattern: Dense report pages, ranked tables and bar charts in his own brand colours, one metric per chart, his photograph on the cover and sales pages.
Funnel mechanic: Free newsletter and a free LinkedIn knowledge quiz at the top, the paid report as the tripwire, then a four-month application-only programme and a membership, plus keynotes. He says 80% of his own conversion comes from newsletters.
Verified figures, edition named: 2025 (via Mercer-Mackay): images with people perform up to 50% better; documents of 8 to 10 slides perform best; text-only company page multiplier 0.28. 2026 (podcast): optimal frequency moved from "5 to 6" to "2 to 4" posts a week; standalone articles 0.4x normal reach. 2026 (via Goodman, May 2026): one external link reduces median reach by 18.8%. Via Blueberry Media: three or more meaningful comments in the first 60 minutes gives about 5.2x reach amplification. Not found in any van der Blom source: format multipliers of 1.39x or 1.30x (those are AuthoredUp's), a 70% golden-hour share, a 360Brew section, a March 2026 engagement-bait clause.
Transfer to AIFC: Take two to four posts a week, 8 to 10 slide documents, people in photographs, no link in the body unless the click is worth more than the reach. Refuse quoting any of his figures as LinkedIn fact: one analyst's sample, edition beside every number.
