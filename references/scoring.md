## How each reference is scored

Five marks out of five, the same five every time.

- **Virality** did this reach beyond the author's own audience
- **Shareability** would a reader forward it to a colleague without adding a caveat
- **Applicability** can we run the same mechanism honestly, with what we actually have
- **Cleanliness** one clear intention, nothing on it that is not doing a job
- **Saveability** is it worth keeping after the feed has moved on

## supplied/

### mindstream-50-claude-use-cases.png
Virality 5, shareability 5, applicability 4, cleanliness 3, saveability 5.
Fifty numbered cards, five columns, colour-coded, one topic covered to exhaustion, a heavy
title bar and a quiet footer. This is the single best format on the board: the reader gets the
whole map in one image and keeps it. Marked down on cleanliness only because ten colours carry
no meaning, so the eye works harder than it needs to.
**Taken:** the grid itself (`../viren/grid.mjs`), the title bar, the numbered chips, the one-line
body, the footer that names where to go next. **Left:** the ten-colour palette; ours uses six
that group the families.

### mindstream-20-dos-and-donts.png
Virality 4, shareability 4, applicability 4, cleanliness 2, saveability 5.
Same house, split into a do half and a do-not half. The split is the idea and it is a good one:
a reader checks themselves against it. The iceberg illustration in the middle fights the
content and forces an uneven grid, which is why cleanliness is low.
**Taken:** the two-column do and do-not structure for a future grid. **Left:** the decorative
centrepiece. Nothing on our sheets that is not doing a job.

### claude-b2b-sales-stack.png
Virality 4, shareability 3, applicability 3, cleanliness 3, saveability 4.
Five stacked layers, each a library with a big number. The instinct is right and the payoff is
wrong: the sheet is a map of a thing you only get by commenting "B2B". That mechanism is banned
in our voice rules, LinkedIn's March 2026 update names it, and it hands the list to the platform
rather than to us.
**Taken:** the layered library with a number per layer. **Left:** the comment gate. Ours gives
the whole thing away and links to a page with no form on it.

### funnel-5-stages.png
Virality 2, shareability 3, applicability 5, cleanliness 4, saveability 3.
Awareness, interest, consideration, conversion, retention. Generic as a post and exactly right
as a working diagram. It is a reminder that a post sits at stage one and nothing else: the post
earns attention, the profile and the site do the converting, and the post never tries to.
**Taken:** the discipline of naming which stage a piece is for. **Left:** publishing it. Nobody
saves a funnel diagram from a founder.

### lara-tweet-card.png
Virality 4, shareability 4, applicability 5, cleanliness 5, saveability 2.
Near-black ground, avatar, name, verified tick, one-line tagline, then two short paragraphs. No
headline, no logo, no decoration. The cleanest card format on the board and the easiest to run
every week.
**Taken:** the whole card, as `tweet` in `../viren/render.mjs`, with Viren's face and tagline.
**Left:** nothing. Saveability is low by design; this format carries a belief, not a reference,
which is why it alternates with the grid rather than replacing it.

## What the five together say

The two formats that earn a save are the dense grid and the real artefact. The format that
earns a share is a person saying one true thing plainly. Run both: the grid and the carousel
carry the value, the card carries the voice, and the photograph carries the week. Nothing in
any of them argues for what we sell.

### ai-powered-leader-online-business-plan.png
762 reactions, 42 comments, 77 reposts. Virality 5, shareability 5, applicability 3,
cleanliness 3, saveability 4.
Six concentric rounded panels, outermost the broadest thinking, working inward to the daily

### nicolas-cole-22-writing-rules-carousel.png
112 reactions, 47 comments. Virality 3, shareability 4, applicability 5, cleanliness 4,
saveability 5.
Twenty-four pages. The cover is a photograph of him in the street with the title overlaid in
yellow, then the slides are flat yellow grounds, each one a numbered bold rule and three short
lines under it. The length works because every page stands alone and the reader can stop
anywhere; it would be fatal if the value were back-loaded.
**Taken:** two things. The hybrid deck, a photograph cover for the stop and designed slides for
the legibility, which we can build today by mixing `photocover` with the existing body slides.
And the contract a number sets: twenty-two rules in the title means twenty-two slides, no
padding, no bonus.
**Left:** the money frame and the link in the body. The rules themselves are the give; his are
wrapped in a crash course.
