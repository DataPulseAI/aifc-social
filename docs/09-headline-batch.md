# 24 headlines in the right register

All from real stories in the week to 15 September 2026. Written to the rule: a named
person or company, a specific number, and a claim that sounds like something you would
repeat to a colleague. Kept as a calibration set, not a queue.

## Tier A: the ones I would run

1. **Google just quietly let all its engineers use a rival's AI**
   The tell is "quietly". Google is a big Anthropic investor and Gemini is its own product.
2. **Anthropic posted a $450,000 sales job aimed at Meta. Then deleted it.**
   The number and the deletion. Two hooks in nine words.
3. **"I have a job teaching people how to use AI. I spend half my time telling people not to."**
   Kristen Swanson at Anthropic. First person, counterintuitive, and it is our entire
   positioning said by someone other than us.
4. **The world's richest man says he's living in a trailer**
   No adjectives, no framing, just the fact.
5. **Oracle's new CFO told staff she hates the phrase "doing more with less"**
   The day after layoffs started. The tension is the story.
6. **Meta spent months asking staff for their keystrokes. The staff won.**
   Flyers, memes, a petition, and the programme was dropped. A rare story where the
   employees beat the AI rollout.
7. **Entry-level tech jobs in New York fell 49% in three years**
   One number, one place, one timeframe.
8. **Meta is asking people it made individual contributors to be managers again**
   Eighteen months after flattening. The reversal is the point.
9. **A Harvard economist found the single best predictor of a pay rise. It's meetings.**
   Everyone hates meetings. That is why this travels.
10. **"I'm a French CEO in NYC. I say no to 80% of my email."**
    Worth stealing the structure of: first person, a number, a refusal.

## What changed in the writing

**Before:** "Six questions to settle before you roll out an AI assistant"
**After:** "I have a job teaching people how to use AI. I spend half my time telling people not to."

**Before:** "Microsoft shipped 11 Copilot changes this fortnight. Three matter to you."
**After:** "Google just quietly let all its engineers use a rival's AI"

**Before:** "The average number of AI technologies in use at a UK business that has adopted any at all."
**After:** "35% of UK firms use AI. The average one uses it for 1.6 things."

Three rules produced the difference.

1. **A person or a company does something.** Not a topic, a subject with a verb. Passive
   constructions and gerunds kill it every time.
2. **One number, and it is surprising.** $450,000. 49%. 80%. 1.6. If there is no number,
   there had better be a quote.
3. **Say the whole thing.** No teasing, no "here's why", no colons. The best example gives
   away the entire story in the headline and people still stop.

The fourth rule is negative: **nothing that sounds like a training company talking.** If
the headline contains "rollout", "adoption", "framework", "guide to" or "what your team
should", it is a blog post, not a news card.
