# 08. The "ours" post: promoting our free resources without reading as an advert

Written 11 October 2026 for the new audience: owners and managers of UK businesses with roughly 5 to 250 staff who are not tech-native. Governs the one post in ten that points at something of ours. Where this disagrees with `docs/16` section 3, this wins on audience and card; `docs/16` still wins on the destination map and the magnet rotation. `docs/01` (voice) and `docs/10` (sourcing) win over both.

Every page described below was opened on 11 October 2026. What each page actually contains is in section 2c. Do not describe a resource from memory or from an older doc: one older doc (`docs/01` on the March 2026 update) and one banked post (`2026-10-01-week-four-promo`) already describe their sources slightly wrong (sections 2b and 5).

---

## 1. Aim, reader moment, metric, frequency

**Aim.** Get a click to `/prompts` or an insights article, or a download of one of the three PDFs, from an owner who already finds the page useful. Do it without costing reach and without spending goodwill. A promo that reads as an advert costs more than it brings: the reader learns to scroll past the wordmark.

**The reader's moment.** A practice manager, an estate agency owner or a site office manager on their phone between jobs. They have seen two or three of our posts and got something from them. They are not shopping for training. They will give us the click only if this post is already useful on its own and the link plainly offers more of the same, free.

**The rule that makes it work** (`docs/16` section 3): give away the substance in the post and on the card, and let the page or PDF be the convenience. The reader who never clicks still leaves with something they can use today. That is what earns the click from the reader who does.

**What we measure, in order.**

| Metric | Where it comes from | Why it is the measure |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks | Buffer engagement on the post, and site sessions with `utm_campaign=<post_id>` (`docs/27` rule 3) | The only thing this post type exists to produce |
| Downloads | Form submissions on `/resources/<slug>` carrying the same UTM | Stage 3 of the funnel in `docs/27`. Expect few, and count them by post |
| Enquiries | `/book` and the contact form, same UTM | Rare. Never the target of a single post. Log one if it happens, do not chase it |
| Guard: reach | Impressions against the other posts that fortnight | If a promo sits at the bottom of the fortnight twice running, check the card first, then the hook, and only then the link |

Our only in-house data point: the 25-prompts utility post on Viren's profile, 23 September 2026, ran 4.3% engagement on 674 impressions, then 3.87% on 802 when re-read on 5 October (`docs/21` section 2). That is one post on one channel. Treat it as a hint that useful-first promos convert attention, not as a benchmark.

**How often.**

- One post in ten (`docs/22` section 2, the "Ours" band). At two posts a weekday, that is one a week.
- A lead magnet no more than once a fortnight, rotating `ai-policy`, `prompt-library`, `week-four` (`docs/16` section 2). The other week's slot goes to `/prompts` or an insights article.
- Send people to the article rather than the magnet about two times in three (`docs/16` section 1). The article does the persuading and carries the magnet at its end.
- The training itself: at most once a month, taking that week's slot. Never on the same day as another own-material post.
- No more than three posts in ten pushing to stage 3 (`docs/27` rule 1). In practice this band is the only one that does.

### 2d. Creators and companies who promote free resources well

1. **Ruben Hassid** (`references/creators/ruben-hassid.md`). Links in 17 of 38 posts, and every one goes to his own free guide. The post is the procedure: numbered steps, imperative, a screenshot of the actual prompt as the image, no question close. The guide is the longer version. His posts are forwarded because a team lead can send them on as they are. **Take:** the post is the procedure; the link is "the rest of them". **Refuse:** the personal-brand frame; we are a page.
2. **Charlie Hills** (`references/creators/hills-and-jain.md`, `references/winners/index.md`). "I've hoarded 100+ Claude repos this year. These are my top 30 (all free, links below)", 2,510 reactions, 4.3x his median. The image is the grouped list itself. Says "all free" in the hook and means it. Admits error in brackets: "(I had four of them wrong)". **Take:** the big real number, the grouped list as the card, "free" stated once and plainly. **Refuse:** the "(finally)" tic and the ask on 21 of 26 posts.
3. **Justin Welsh** (`references/creators/justin-welsh.md`; [Justin Welsh, My million dollar social media sales funnel, 14 October 2023](https://www.justinwelsh.me/newsletter/my-million-dollar-social-media-sales-funnel)). The post is a complete lesson; nothing depends on the click. "Half an hour after I share a piece of content, I follow-up with a link to an article on my website", and that article goes deeper on the same point, not to a generic page. **Take:** the value-complete post, and linking to the article that goes deeper on exactly that point rather than the homepage. **Refuse:** a separate follow-up carrying the link (if it is a comment it is deboosted, and first comments are not available on our plan) and the revenue flexing.
4. **Amanda Natividad, SparkToro** ([Zero-Click Content, 1 August 2022](https://amandanat.substack.com/p/zero-click-content-how-to-beat-the)). Gives the punchline away in the promotion itself; her webinar email that gave away the joke's punchline became "our most-clicked promo email of the year". Summarises the long piece natively and links to the full one. **Take:** give the payoff first. **Refuse:** her "withhold the last item of the list" tactic. A list with a hole in it is a teaser, and the owner rejected teasers.
5. **Section, Greg Shove** (`references/creators/section-greg-shove.md`). The closest business to ours: AI upskilling sold to companies, with original research as the magnet. One figure per post, then the diagnosis, then an explicit bridge to the paid benchmark. **Take:** the bridge from a free self-check to the paid thing, used rarely and stated plainly. **Refuse:** gating the knowledge itself behind a HubSpot form, and the crisis framing ("The AI Proficiency Crisis"), which is exactly what makes our reader feel stupid.

The counterexample is **Chris Donnelly** (`references/creators/chris-donnelly.md`): "I'm giving the full set away for free" with 2,353 comments and 3,090 on another, because every comment is the price of the resource. It works at 1.2M followers. On a small page talking to owners who already feel behind, it reads as a trick, and it is banned in `docs/01`.

### 2e. What we take and what we refuse

**Take.** The usable thing on the card (a full prompt, the actual clauses, the actual three numbers). A real count of what is on the page ("25 prompts, no form"). One link, in the body, last line, deep-linked to the exact prompt or article. "Free" said once, plainly, because it is true and it is the reader's first question. The article as the default destination. Carousels for the magnets.

**Refuse.**
- Comment-to-get, DM-for-the-link, "drop a YES".
- Fake scarcity: "only this week", "before we take it down", "limited copies". The resources are permanent and free.
- Download counts, "most opened", "most popular", "teams love". We do not publish usage numbers, so we cannot say them.
- Testimonials, "our clients", "teams we work with", "in our experience". We have no delivery history.
- Teaser cards that list titles of what is inside instead of the thing itself.
- "Link in the comments."
- Any description of a PDF's contents beyond what its page lists.

## 3. The production prompt

````text
You are writing one LinkedIn post for the AI for Companies company page: the "ours" post,
which points at one of our free resources (or, rarely, the training). The reader is an
owner or manager of a UK business with 5 to 250 staff: a dental practice, an estate agent,
an accountant, a builders' merchant, a cafe, a charity, a trade. They have heard of
ChatGPT, may have tried it, may pay for Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace. They are not
technical and slightly intimidated. They must never feel stupid, lectured or sold to.
Write at about reading age 12. Contractions are fine.

INPUTS (fill all of them before writing)
- destination: one of /prompts#<anchor>, /insights/<slug>, /resources/<slug>, /training
- page_notes: what the destination page contains, copied from the page as opened TODAY.
  Not from memory, not from docs/16, not from an older post. If you have not opened the
  page in this run, stop and open it.
- the_thing: the one usable thing you will give away whole (a prompt, the clauses, the
  three numbers). It must come from page_notes or the article that page links to.
- reader_moment: the specific moment in an owner's week this fixes ("a complaint just
  came in", "the licence renews next month", "someone asked if we have an AI policy").
- last_ours: date and destination of the last two ours posts (from bank/ledger.csv).
  Refuse to write if the same magnet ran in the last 14 days, or another own-material
  post is scheduled the same day.
- card_type: prompt (for a single prompt), cheatsheet (3 to 6 items you can use as they
  are), or carousel (a magnet's full framework, 6 to 9 slides).

THE SHAPE (four parts, from docs/16, adapted)
1. The moment, in the reader's words. First line under about 80 characters, ideally
   under 40. A flat statement of the situation or of what they get. Not a question, not
   our name, not "most people".
2. The thing itself, most of it. On the card in full, and in the body the two or three
   parts that do the work, each with one plain sentence on why. This is 70 to 80 percent
   of the caption. If the reader stopped here, they could act today.
3. One sentence naming the resource, plainly, with what is really in it and that it is
   free, then the link. Count only what the page shows: "25 prompts, no form", "three
   pages: seven clauses, four decisions to make first". Nothing the page does not list.
4. Stop. No question, no urgency, no "hope this helps". If the post touches UK GDPR,
   personal data, employment or contracts, the very last line is: This is not legal
   advice.

TITLE FORMULAS (pick one for the card title and adapt the first line from it; write
three, in different formulas, then choose)
1. The moment, then the result: "A complaint just came in. Get a calm, honest reply drafted."
2. What to type when: "What to type when two quotes don't match."
3. All of it, here: "A one-page AI policy for a small business. All seven parts."
4. The question they are asked: "Someone asked if you have an AI policy. Here's one page."
5. The count before you pay: "Before the licence renews, count these three things."
6. The free number: "25 prompts for everyday jobs. No form. Start with this one."
7. Is it working: "Is the team actually using AI? Three numbers at week four."
8. The job they hate: "The monthly report, drafted from your own notes."
Rules: under 12 words; say the job, not the tool's features; no "ultimate", "complete
guide", "toolkit", "framework", "hack", "secret"; no colon-then-hype.

HOW MUCH TO GIVE AWAY
- /prompts: the whole prompt on the card, word for word from the page (trimmed to under
  60 words only by cutting, never by adding). Never tease the prompt.
- A magnet: the whole framework (every clause name with what it says, all three numbers
  with how to collect them). The PDF adds the blank sheet, the worked example, the
  decisions, the print-ready layout. Say that is what the PDF adds.
- An article: its main answer in full; the link is "the long version, with sources".
- The training: what the day is, who it is for, what people leave with, the guarantee as
  the page words it. No price (the page gives none). No outcomes, no testimonials.

LINK
- Exactly one link, in the body, on its own line or ending the third part. Never in a
  comment, never two links.
- Deep link to the exact thing: /prompts#complaint-response, not /prompts.
- Prefer the insights article over the magnet about two times in three.
- Write the bare URL (aiforcompanies.co.uk/...). queue.mjs adds UTM. Do not add it by hand.

LENGTH
- Prompt card or cheatsheet post: 700 to 1,200 characters.
- Carousel post: under 400 characters (hook, one line, the resource line, the legal line
  if needed). The carousel carries the substance.
- Training post: 800 to 1,200 characters, text only or a cheatsheet of what the day covers.

CARD (output exactly one, as JSON, matching the shared schemas)
- prompt: {"template":"prompt","title":"","prompt":"","attach":"","tools":["ChatGPT","Copilot","Gemini"]}
  Only list tools the page says the prompt is for, or "any assistant" ones as all three.
- cheatsheet: {"template":"cheatsheet","title":"","items":[{"label":"","value":""}],"source":""}
  3 to 6 items. label 2 to 5 words, the situation. value up to 22 words, the usable thing.
  source = the page and its date, e.g. "aiforcompanies.co.uk/insights, 3 September 2026".
- carousel: {"template":"carousel","slides":[{"kind":"cover|step|end","title":"","body":""}]}
  6 to 9 slides. Cover: the title formula, body one line. Steps: one item each, title is
  the item's name, body up to 22 words of what it says. End: what the PDF adds and the
  bare URL, nothing else; the renderer adds the byline and one CTA.
Card law: the card alone gives the reader something usable. Title and value only. No
URL on prompt or cheatsheet cards (the link is in the body). No "free download" banner,
no "link below", no arrows.

OUTPUT
1. Three candidate first lines in different formulas, then the chosen one.
2. The post body.
3. The card JSON.
4. Bank fields: destination, stage (2 for /prompts and articles, 3 for /resources, 3 for
   /training), source_publisher=AIFC, source_url = the page you opened, opened date.
5. The self-check, answered line by line.

SELF-CHECK (any "no" means rewrite before output)
[ ] Does the card give something usable to someone who never reads the caption?
[ ] Could a reader act today without clicking?
[ ] Is every word describing the resource on its page as opened today? Nothing invented
    about the PDF's contents?
[ ] Exactly one link, in the body, deep-linked, no UTM typed by hand?
[ ] First line under 80 characters, no question, no "most people", not our name?
[ ] None of: comment/DM/save this/follow, "limited", "only", "grab", "don't miss",
    download counts, "most popular", "our clients", "in our experience", "teams love"?
[ ] No jargon: agentic, LLM, tenant, workflow automation, use case, leverage, roadmap,
    transformation, governance framework, upskilling?
[ ] Tools named plainly ("ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini, whichever you have")?
[ ] UK English, no em dashes, no double hyphens, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no
    hashtags, no italics, "we" never "I", dates as 20 July 2026?
[ ] If personal data, UK GDPR, employment or contracts are touched, the last line is
    "This is not legal advice."?
[ ] Would an owner forward this to a colleague even if we deleted the last paragraph?
[ ] Not the same magnet within 14 days, not a second own-material post that day?
````

### 4a. `/prompts`: the complaint reply

Page facts used: prompt "Reply to a complaint properly", Client service, any assistant, anchor `#complaint-response`. Attach: the complaint with personal data removed, and an honest account of what happened. The prompt asks for a reply that acknowledges the problem in their words, explains in two sentences without blaming a colleague or a system, states what we will do and by when, offers one way to reach me, avoids "we take this very seriously", stays under 180 words, and marks anything to confirm with [CHECK]. What good looks like, verbatim: "A reply you would be happy for the client to forward to their boss."

Candidate first lines: "A complaint just came in. Here's what to type." (moment then result) / "What to type when a customer complains." (what to type when) / "25 prompts, no form. Start with the complaint one." (the free number). Chosen: the first.

**Card**

```json
{"template":"prompt","title":"A complaint just came in. Get a calm, honest reply drafted.","prompt":"Below is a complaint and my notes on what happened. Draft a reply under 180 words that names their problem in their words, explains what happened in two sentences without blaming anyone, says what we will do and by when, and gives one way to reach me. No \"we take this very seriously\". Mark anything to check with [CHECK].","attach":"The complaint with names and contact details taken out, plus your own notes on what happened.","tools":["ChatGPT","Copilot","Gemini"]}
```

The card prompt is 58 words, cut down from the page's version without adding anything. The full wording is one tap away at the anchor.

**Post** (about 1,050 characters)

> A complaint just came in. Here's what to type.
>
> Open ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini, whichever you have. Paste in the complaint with the customer's name and contact details taken out. Add a few lines of your own on what actually happened. Then use the prompt on the card.
>
> Three bits of it do the work. "In their words" makes the reply show you read what they wrote. "Without blaming anyone" stops the draft pointing at a colleague or "the system", which a customer reads as an excuse. And [CHECK] marks any date or promise you need to confirm before it goes, so you don't commit to something you can't deliver.
>
> Then read the draft as the customer would. A good test: would you be happy for them to forward it to their boss? If yes, send it.
>
> It's one of 25 prompts for everyday jobs, each with what to attach and what a good answer looks like. No form, nothing to sign up for.
> aiforcompanies.co.uk/prompts#complaint-response
>
> This is not legal advice.

Bank: destination `/prompts#complaint-response`, stage 2. The legal line is there because the post tells the reader to remove personal data.

## 5. Before and after: `2026-10-01-week-four-promo`

### Before

Card: a stock photograph of a man at a whiteboard in a concrete room, the AIforCompanies wordmark at the top, and one line over the photo: "Three numbers to count four weeks after any AI training". The photo carries no information and the line is a teaser: the card does not say what the three numbers are.

Body (as banked):

> Three numbers to count four weeks after any AI training. All three here in full, because the point of this is that you can do it without us.
>
> One. How many people opened the tool in the last seven days. [...]
> Two. How many of the tasks you named at the start are now being done with it. [...]
> Three. How much time the team says it got back, asked as a question about one specific task rather than as a satisfaction score. [...]
>
> The worked version, with a sheet you can print, is at aiforcompanies.co.uk/insights/measure-ai-adoption-three-numbers

### What is wrong with it, for the new reader

1. **The card fails the card law.** It teases the three numbers instead of giving them. Someone who only sees the image gains nothing.
2. **"After any AI training" assumes a purchase the reader has not made.** Most of our new readers switched ChatGPT or Copilot on and hoped. They have not bought training. The post talks past them.
3. **The three numbers do not match the article it links to.** The article (3 September 2026) names minutes saved on named tasks, workflows still in weekly use, and people who used the tool for a real task this week. The post names logins in seven days, named tasks, and time back. A reader who clicks finds a different list. That is the kind of drift that makes a page look careless.
4. **"Without us" is about us.** It is a nice line for a reader who already knows we sell training. For an owner who does not, it raises a question the post never answers.
5. **It leaves out the decision.** The article's most useful part for an owner is what to do with the numbers: extend, fix what blocked it, or stop. The post stops at diagnosis.

### After

Candidate first lines: "Is the team actually using AI? Three numbers at week four." / "Paying for ChatGPT or Copilot? Count these three at week four." / "Four weeks in, three numbers tell you if AI stuck." Chosen: the third as the post's first line, the first as the card title.

**Card**

```json
{"template":"cheatsheet","title":"Is the team actually using AI? Count these three at week four.","items":[
{"label":"Minutes saved, named jobs","value":"Pick the five jobs you hoped AI would help with. Ask how long each took this week. Compare with before."},
{"label":"Jobs still done with it","value":"Count the things people started doing with AI in week one. How many did anyone do that way this week?"},
{"label":"Real use this week","value":"Ask in the team meeting who used it on real work this week. Work that went somewhere, not logins."},
{"label":"Then decide, in writing","value":"Roll it out to the next team, fix what got in the way, or stop. Write it beside the numbers."}
],"source":"aiforcompanies.co.uk/insights, 3 September 2026"}
```

**Post** (about 1,150 characters)

> Four weeks in, three numbers tell you if AI stuck.
>
> This works whether your team was trained, watched a video, or just got a login. You don't need a dashboard. The tool's own figures count people who are signed in, and being signed in isn't the same as using it.
>
> One. Minutes saved on named jobs. Pick the five jobs you hoped AI would help with and ask how long each took this week. If you never took a before figure, take one today and count again in four weeks.
>
> Two. Jobs still done with it. Count the things people started doing with AI in the first week, then how many anyone did that way this week. The ones that survived tell you what mattered.
>
> Three. Real use. Ask in the team meeting who used it on real work this week, meaning work that went somewhere.
>
> Then make one decision and write it down next to the numbers: roll it out to the next team, fix what got in the way, or stop.
>
> The long version, with a sheet to print, is here:
> aiforcompanies.co.uk/insights/measure-ai-adoption-three-numbers

Bank: destination `/insights/measure-ai-adoption-three-numbers` (the article, not the magnet: this is a two-in-three cycle), stage 2. Note the body says "watched a video, or just got a login" as our adaptation of an article written about training days; the three measures themselves are the article's.

## 6. Fail list: eight ways a promo goes salesy or corporate

| # | Failure | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The teaser card | A photo with "Three numbers to count", or a grid of titles ("THE POLICY NOBODY READS") with a vague line under each. The rejected six-jobs grid and the old week-four card | Put the usable thing on the card: the whole prompt, the actual clauses, the actual numbers. If it would fit in a table of contents, it is a teaser |
| 2 | Describing the resource wrongly | The old week-four promo listed three numbers that are not the article's three | Open the page in the same run. Describe only what it lists. Count only what you counted |
| 3 | Claims we cannot back | "The three that get opened most" (`2026-10-05-prompts-reuse-promo`), "teams love it", "our most popular", "most teams that do this end up using two of the five" | We publish no usage data and have no delivery history. Cut the claim, or replace it with what the page shows ("25 prompts, no form") |
| 4 | The advert opener | "We've just published...", "Introducing our free...", "AI for Companies is proud to..." | Open with the reader's moment ("A complaint just came in") or with the thing itself. Our name belongs in the post header, which LinkedIn already shows |
| 5 | Corporate vocabulary | "A comprehensive AI governance framework to empower your organisation's adoption journey", "toolkit", "upskill", "use case" | Use the owner's words: "a one-page AI policy", "what to type", "is anyone using it". Read it aloud as if to a practice manager |
| 6 | Gating by stealth | "Comment POLICY", "DM us for the link", "link in the comments", withholding the last item so the click is forced | One link, in the body, to a page where the knowledge is free and only the PDF asks for a name and email |
| 7 | Manufactured urgency | "Only this week", "before it's gone", "don't miss out", "grab yours", "limited" | The resources are permanent and free. End on the resource line, or on "This is not legal advice." where it applies, and stop |
| 8 | Pitching the training on the back of a free post | A prompt post that ends "Want your team to do this properly? Book a call", or a second link to /book | The free post stays free. The training gets its own post at most once a month: what the day is, who it is for, what people leave with, the guarantee in the page's words, no price, no outcomes |

Two smaller ones that recur: stacking more than one link (the reader picks neither), and adding a question close to a promo ("Which would you try first?"), which turns a useful post into a survey and is the shape `docs/01` section 6 already refuses.
