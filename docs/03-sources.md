the bottom so the task does not waste a run rediscovering them.

## Images: the rule

> **Getty is not royalty free.** See `07-feeds-and-detection.md` section 4. Their free
> embed tool is a web-page iframe, not a downloadable image, and it cannot be used here.


**We never use an image from a news site, a competitor, a stock site we have not
licensed, or a search result.** Business Insider's images are licensed from Getty or
shot by their staff. Lifting one is copyright infringement, it exposes DataPulse AI
Limited to a claim, and takedowns on LinkedIn are automated and fast. Crediting the
source in the caption is not a licence and does not change this.

What we do instead, in order of preference:

1. **Rendered brand cards.** Our own typography on our own backdrops, generated by
   `lib/render.mjs`. This is the default and covers the large majority of posts. It is
   also what the best accounts in this category actually do: Donnelly and Mistry post
   designed graphics, not photography.
2. **Procedural backdrops.** `lib/backdrop.mjs` generates a deterministic abstract
   ground in four tones, seeded from the headline. Legally clean, infinitely available,
   and consistent with the brand.
3. **A seeded photo library.** A folder of images we hold a licence for, sourced once
   and reused. Unsplash and Pexels both permit commercial use without attribution;
   Wikimedia Commons and Openverse carry per-item CC terms that must be recorded.
   Store the licence and attribution alongside every file in `photos/index.json`.
   This container cannot reach those sites, so the library is seeded manually and grown
   over time rather than fetched at run time.
4. **Vendor logos via Brandfetch**, which is already connected, for posts about a named
   product. Brandfetch supplies these for exactly this use.
5. **Our own photography**, once the site's Pexels placeholders are replaced.

**Linking to the source article is not only allowed, it is the point.** Summarise in
our words, credit the publication by name and date in the card's credit line, and link
to them. That sends them readers and costs us nothing. What we take is the fact, which
is not copyrightable. What we never take is their picture or their prose.
