# 23. What the creators do, and what we take from it

Written 21 September 2026 from a read of 21 accounts: the twenty in Josh Sanders' "20
accounts will teach you more than a degree" list and Lara Acosta, whom Viren named first.
Roughly 500 posts were read through Viren's own LinkedIn session, with reactions, comments,
reposts, format, length and closing shape recorded for each. The per-creator notes are in
`bank/creators/`. This doc is the conclusions. `docs/22` still governs the mix; this refines
it and adds the pillar model and the personal-profile track.

One fact frames everything below. **The page has 8 followers.** It reached 460 people in
its second week through hashtags and the feed, not through an audience. Every account
studied here is a person with 50k to 2M followers. Their formats transfer. Their
distribution does not, and no format fixes that.

Three findings hold across the whole set.

**News is the weakest thing anyone posts.** Rowan Cheung runs the largest AI newsletter in
the world and his median LinkedIn post is 150 reactions. His top news posts are the ones
where he used the thing ("I got early access, here are 9 use cases", "the agent built me a
retirement plan"). A bare announcement, Veo 2, sits at 285 with two million readers behind
it. Alex Banks' news works when it carries one sentence of interpretation the reader could
not have written ("enterprises wouldn't touch Grok, so it bought the tool they already
use"). Our page was built on the weakest format in the study without anyone's audience.

**What gets forwarded is a procedure or a list a colleague can use.** Hassid, Hills and
Donnelly take 100 to 340 reposts per top post. Every one of those posts is either numbered
steps for one task ("pick one tool, one weekly task, define what good looks like") or a
grouped library with a big number ("30 things to install, grouped by what they do"). Reposts
are the mechanism by which a post reaches people who do not follow you, and for a page with
8 followers that is the only mechanism there is.

**What gets talked about is a person, a position or a correction.** Comments cluster on
first-person stories (Lara, Heather), on claims someone can argue with (Isenberg's "the only
businesses left to build", Donnelly's "the average millionaire is 61"), and on corrections of
a belief the reader holds ("Copilot is not its own model", "SEO is not just ranking",
"Nobody buys on LinkedIn because they liked your post"). Nobody comments on a changelog.

## 2. What we take, and what we refuse

**Take.** The procedure post (Hassid). The grouped library with a number (Hills). The
misconception opener (Donnelly, Heather). The original count (Grandillon: "I checked 49
tools, 46 do X"). News plus numbered interpretation (Isenberg: "I just read X. What I think
it means: 1, 2, 3"). The hands-on release read (Cheung: "we tried it on a Monday task, here
is what happened"). The text-only opinion post (Isenberg, 601 reactions, no image, no link).
The weekly "three people, three ideas" that names and tags who said it (York). Two posts a
week from a real face (every account in the top half of the table).

**Refuse.** The "Most people/founders + wrong habit" opener, the one-sentence-per-line
stack, the question close on every post, and the identical post run on two accounts: the
Village and Biddlecombe operation posts word-for-word duplicates across two profiles and
re-runs them a week later. It works for them at 170 reactions a post and it is the most
recognisable AI-written shape on the platform. Two accounts in the roster (Cook, Hassid)
had their best posts of the month by attacking exactly that shape, and LinkedIn readers can
now flag a post as "seems like AI slop". The voice rules in `docs/01` are commercial rules
now, not taste. Also refused: comment-to-get-the-resource (Donnelly's 3,090-comment posts),
banned in `docs/01` and named by LinkedIn's March 2026 update, however well it still works
for a 1.2M account.

## 3. The pillars: inform, entertain, retain

The brief asked for the pillars. Nader Alnajjar's post on Chris Donnelly's method gives the
industry's own version (seven types: awareness, education, personal story, proof of work,
building in public, lead magnet, newsjacking) and the rule that matters: **every post has
its own goal, and you need them in rotation, not all every week.** Mapped to the three
pillars and to the bands in `docs/22`:

| Pillar | Job | Bands in `docs/22` | What the creators showed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inform** | Reach people who do not follow us; earn the repost | Wide, vendor (as roundup) | Interpretation beats summary. Procedure beats explanation. A count nobody else has beats a stat everyone has |
| **Entertain** | Make someone stop, and make them react | Wide (tension, a number that sounds wrong), position | Satire with a serious point (Banks' agent workflow, 1,414). The overheard dialogue. The claim you could argue with |
| **Retain** | Give the people who already read us a reason to come back and eventually book | Do-this-Monday, ours, and the personal-profile track below | A named recurring format (SLAY, "the one thing machine", "3 people, 3 ideas"). A library the posts keep pointing back to. A face they recognise |

Two things about retention that the page cannot do and the creators all do: proof of work
and building in public (Alnajjar's types 4 and 5). We have no delivery history, so there is
no proof of work to post, and a company page building in public reads as a startup diary.
Both are open to Viren's profile: "here is what we changed on the site this week and why",
"here is the session plan we wrote for a 30-person accountancy and what we got wrong". That
is the retention pillar, and it lives on the profile.

## 4. Repeatable formats, named, with a slot

Repeatability is the point: a reader should recognise the format before reading it, and the
batch should be able to write each one from a template. Seven, one per weekday slot plus two
floating:

| Format | Slot | Shape | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Monday count** | Mon AM | "We checked N things. M of them do X." An original count we did ourselves, about the buyer's stack, one paragraph of what it means | Grandillon |
| **One task, one hour** | Tue AM | A procedure for one weekly task with one assistant: what to attach, what to type, what good looks like. Numbered. Ends. | Hassid |
| **The correction** | Wed AM | "People think X. It is Y." One misconception a UK buyer holds, corrected with a source | Donnelly, Heather |
| **The position** | Thu AM | A claim someone could argue with, argued, no link | Isenberg |
| **The week, read** | Fri AM | The roundup: vendor changes plus one UK document, each with a why line, names and tags the publishers | York, Banks |
| **The library** | floating, twice a month | A grouped list with a big number, drawn from /prompts, the sector pages, the guides. The card is the list | Hills |
| **The story** | floating, when there is one | A named person or institution did something surprising. Portrait or text-only. One interpretive sentence | Banks, Cheung |

Afternoon slots take the wide story when there is one that clears `docs/22` section 6, or
a second procedure. Nothing else. Every format ends without a question unless the question
is one only a real reader can answer (`docs/01` section 6).

## 5. The personal-profile track

This is the change that would do the most and the one nothing in the engine can do alone.
Donnelly's own explanation of his $5m: "several team members posting content builds a human
brand that people can follow". Lara: "people buy from people they trust". Heather Murray, our
closest competitor, runs a £1m training business from a profile whose top posts are an award,
a joke, a team photo and a confession that she has never used Claude Code. The page can be
the engine. The profile has to be the front.

Concretely: one post a day on Viren's profile, weekdays, drawn from the same bank, in the
first person, and carrying the two pillars the page cannot: building in public and proof of
work as it arrives. The page's best post of each day gets reshared from the profile inside
the first hour with one sentence of his own (`docs/20` puts an edited reshare at 3x a bare
one). Buffer can hold his profile as a second channel so the batch schedules both.

The profile is his, so the voice rules bend: "I" is allowed there, and the story of leaving
JP Morgan to do this is exactly the type-3 post every top account runs. Everything else in
`docs/01` holds: no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no engagement bait, every number sourced.

## 6. Cards, from what the templates showed

Sixteen top-performing images were opened. The ones that travelled fall into four kinds, and
none of them is a photograph of an empty office:

1. **A plain list on a plain ground.** Hassid's 1,306-reaction post carried a black list on
   white, "Things in AI that didn't exist five months ago", Arial, no design at all. The
   information was the design.
2. **A grouped list with a big number,** dense, dark, code-editor styled (Hills), or a
   pyramid of banned words (Hassid's "You sound like AI", 2,617).
3. **The infographic map** (Donnelly, Village, Mistry). Dense, saveable, reposted, rarely
   read in feed. Our `grid` and `roundup` templates already do a calmer version of this.
4. **A photograph of the person** (Lara, Heather, Miller). The only kind that needs a face.

Our house card (photo plus one sentence) is a fifth kind that none of the top posts used.
It reads as a publication, which was the intent, and it carries no forwardable information,
which is the problem. Keep it for the story format. Everything else runs on the grid,
roundup and a new plain-list template.

## 7. What changes in the engine

- `docs/22` mix stands. The formats in section 4 become the templates the batch writes from,
  one file each in `bank/formats/` (to write), with the hook family, the shape, the close and
  a worked example.
- A `plainlist` template in `lib/templates.mjs`: headline, up to twelve one-line items, no
  boxes, stone ground, the wordmark small at the foot. For the library and the Monday count.
- `bank/mentions.csv` gets used: the Friday roundup tags each publisher it names.
- The weekly pass scores by format and pillar, not only by archetype and hook. `docs/24`.
- A second Buffer channel for Viren's profile, and a `channel` column in the bank.
