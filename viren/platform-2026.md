# What the platform rewards, September 2026

Checked 21 September 2026 against two independent write-ups of 2026 data. Where they agree the
figure is below; where only one carries a number it says so. This supersedes anything in
`formats.md` that conflicts with it.

Sources opened: dataslayer.ai, LinkedIn algorithm February 2026, drawing on Richard van der
Blom's Algorithm Insights report and their own testing. inspiredmarketingb2b.com, what B2B
LinkedIn content gets engagement in 2026, drawing on 1.3 million business posts.

## The numbers

| Format | Engagement |
|---|---|
| Native document, the PDF carousel | 6.6% to 7% |
| Native video, 30 to 90 seconds | 5.6% |
| Multi-image | 6.5% |
| Single image and text | 3.2% |
| Text only | 2.0% |
| Poll | 0.07%, effectively dead since the March 2026 authenticity update |

Platform-wide average across the 1.3 million post sample: 5.2%.

## The four changes we made because of it

**1. No link in the post body.** A post carrying an external link reaches roughly 60% fewer
people than the same post without one. Both sources say it, and it is the cheapest mistake on
this list to fix. Every link now goes in the first comment instead. Applied to all thirteen
scheduled posts on 21 September.

**2. The post has to work with the image closed.** You cannot copy text out of a picture, and
most people never open one. Every prompt card now quotes its prompt in full in the body, marked
"Here is the prompt in full. Copy it." The card is the artefact; the text is the give.

**3. Weight the mix towards documents.** A carousel earns roughly twice what a single image
does. We have the renderer and we are barely using it. From week four the target mix is one
document a week, not one a fortnight.

**4. Dwell time, not likes.** The measure is how long someone stays, not how fast they tap. A
post read for thirty seconds beats one with fifty quick likes. This is why the dense grid and
the comparison sheet work: they make people stop and read rather than scroll and approve.

## Two things worth doing that we are not doing

**Video, at 5.6%.** Nothing on the profile is video and the format sits second on the table. A
ninety-second answer to one question, filmed on a phone, needs no production. It is the highest
value unbuilt thing we have.

**A LinkedIn newsletter.** It bypasses the feed entirely and pushes a notification to every
subscriber for every edition. That is the owned audience the funnel has wanted since the start,
except it lives on the platform where the audience already is and it costs nothing to run. One
edition a fortnight, built from the same material as the posts.

## Timing

The first 60 to 90 minutes decide distribution, and only about 5% of posts that underperform in
that window recover later. Replying to comments inside the first hour matters more than
anything in the post itself. That is an argument for posting when Viren is actually at a desk,
not at 22:56.
