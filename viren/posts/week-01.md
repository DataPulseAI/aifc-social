## Tue 22 Sep. Value. Contrarian hook. Card: `2026-09-22-demo.png`

Most AI training is a demo.

Somebody stands at the front, shows a tool doing something clever, and everyone nods. A week later nobody has opened it. Nobody has ever learned anything from a demo.

People learn one way: on their own work, with someone sitting next to them. So here is a training session you can run for your team on Monday without paying anyone.

1. Pick one person and one task they do every week. The supplier statement. The weekly report. The notes from the client call.
2. Open their real file, not an example.
3. Type one plain sentence into whichever assistant you already pay for: "Show me every line that appears in one of these lists and not the other."
4. Check the answer together. Where it is wrong, say why, and try again.
5. Save the sentence somewhere they will find it next Monday.

That is the whole session. Twenty minutes. Same time next week.

## Thu 24 Sep. Value. Statement hook. Card: `2026-09-24-who-opened-it.png`

Every company I meet pays for AI.

Almost none of them can tell me how many people actually opened it last month. Not because the number is hidden. Every one of these tools gives whoever runs your IT a report with a name, a date and a count. Nobody has asked for it.

So ask this week. Three steps, an hour, no budget.

1. Ask whoever manages your accounts for the usage report. Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, all of them have one.
2. Count the people who used it on four or more days in the last month. That is your real number. Write it as "9 of 40", not a percentage.
3. Ask those nine what they use it for. Three of them will describe the same task.

That task is your training plan. It is already proven on your own work, by your own people, and it cost you an hour to find.

(149 words)

## Fri 25 Sep. Personal and useful. Statement hook. Card: `2026-09-25-your-story.png`

Nobody asks what your story is.

Everyone asks what you do. You say the sentence you always say, they say theirs, and both of you have forgotten each other by the time the drinks run out.

At Convo #01, a Monday night in King's Cross, we asked a room of founders one question instead: what is your story? Not the pitch. The story.

One person said: "Nobody has ever actually asked me that." I heard it more than once.

If you go to anything this week, try it. Two minutes in, once, and then listen. You will hear where they grew up, what they walked away from, why this and not something else.

Then write one line about them before you leave the room. That line is the difference between someone you met and someone you remember.

(140 words)

## Mon 28 Sep. Useful. Statement hook. Card: `2026-09-28-same-night.png` (light)

The follow-up I send the same night, every time.

Four lines, and the person on the other end never has to work out who I am.

"Good to meet you at [the place]. You mentioned [the thing they said]. Here is what I promised. Coffee on the 3rd?"

Where you met, so they can place you. Their words, so they know you listened. The thing you promised, so the message is a favour and not an ask. One specific date, so it does not become "let's keep in touch", which means never.

It goes the same night because by Friday the conversation is gone for both of you. I have tested that more times than I would like.

A pen and a phone are enough for this. It is also the exact thing Convu does from a ten-second voice note, which is why it exists.

(143 words)
