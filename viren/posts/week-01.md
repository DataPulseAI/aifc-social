## 1. Tue 23 Sep. Statement hook. Card: `2026-09-23-84.png`

84% of UK workers had no AI training last year.

Not "not enough". None. That is the government's own survey of people in work, published in January. In the same survey, 21% said they feel confident using AI at work, and 21% said it has made them more productive.

So here is the picture inside most companies I meet. The licences were bought. An email went round. A few people worked it out on their own and the rest quietly went back to doing things the old way, because nobody showed them, and asking felt like admitting something.

You do not need a budget to change that this week. You need twenty minutes and one person.

1. Pick someone on your team and one task they do every week. The supplier statement, the weekly report, the notes from the Monday call.
2. Sit next to them. Open their real file, not an example.
3. Type one plain sentence into whichever assistant you already pay for. "Show me every line that appears in one of these lists and not the other." "Turn these notes into owners and dates."
4. Check the answer together. Where it is wrong, say why, and try again.
5. Save the sentence somewhere they will find it next Monday.

That is the whole session. Do it with one person this week and two next week. The 84% is not a skills problem. It is a nobody-sat-next-to-me problem, and that one is cheap to fix.

Source: AI Skills for Life and Work, general public survey findings, DCMS and DSIT, 28 January 2026. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-skills-for-life-and-work-general-public-survey-findings/ai-skills-for-life-and-work-general-public-survey-findings

## 2. Wed 24 Sep. Contrarian hook. Card: `2026-09-24-skip.png`

Do not train your managers first.

Every AI rollout I see starts the same way. The senior people get the pilot, the licences and the lunch-and-learn. The people who process the invoices, chase the debtors and answer the phones get it later, if ever. It feels sensible. The research says it is backwards.

A randomised study published in February gave 1,174 adults a business problem to solve, half with AI and half without. Without AI, people with more education beat people with less by a wide margin. With AI, three quarters of that gap disappeared.

Read that as a manager. The biggest lift from these tools goes to the people you were going to skip. The person who has never been sent on a course. The one who left school at sixteen and runs your whole back office from memory. Give them the tool and twenty minutes on their own work and they move further than anyone in the building.

So this week, when you decide who gets the next seat or the next hour of help, invert the list. Start at the bottom of the org chart, not the top. Put the most senior person in the room beside them, not in front of them.

You will get two things. A team where the least credentialed people are suddenly the most useful with the new tool, and a set of senior people who have watched it happen and cannot unsee it.

Source: Cruces, Fernández Meijide, Galiani, Gálvez and Lombardi, Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps? NBER Working Paper 34851, February 2026. https://www.nber.org/papers/w34851

## 4. Fri 26 Sep. Statement hook. Card: `2026-09-26-mirror.png`

Your team uses AI exactly as much as you do in front of them.

Microsoft surveyed 20,000 people who use AI at work, published in May. Two numbers from it changed how I think about rollouts.

First: whether AI actually helps someone is explained twice as much by their organisation (the culture, their manager, how the place works) as by the individual (their attitude, their habits). 67% to 32%. The "some people just get it" theory is mostly wrong.

Second: where managers visibly used AI themselves, their people reported a 17-point lift in the value they got from it. Not managers who approved it, or bought it, or sent the email. Managers who used it where the team could see.

And only one in four people said their leadership was clearly aligned on AI at all.

So the cheapest thing you can do this week costs nothing and takes place in a meeting you already have.

1. Bring one piece of your own work into the room that you did with the assistant. The board summary, the reply to the difficult client, the reconciliation.
2. Show the sentence you typed. Say what it got wrong the first time.
3. Ask one person to bring theirs next week.

That is it. Not a policy, not a programme. A manager doing the thing in the open, once a week, until it is normal.

Source: Microsoft 2026 Work Trend Index, 5 May 2026. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization

## 5. Mon 29 Sep. Statement hook. Card: `2026-09-29-two-percent.png`

54% of small firms use AI. 2% plan to train anyone.

The British Chambers of Commerce asked 668 UK firms in March, nearly all of them small. Just over half use AI, up from a quarter two years ago. Two per cent plan significant investment in AI training.

That gap is the whole story of the next two years for small British companies. The tools are bought. The people are guessing.

There was a third number in the same report, and it is the one I would put in front of a nervous team. 95% of the firms using everyday AI tools said it had made no difference to headcount. The fear that stalls most rollouts, that this is the first step to fewer jobs, is not what the firms who did it report.

If you run a team, here is what I would do with those three numbers on Monday.

1. Say the 95% out loud, in a meeting. Most people are waiting to hear it and nobody has said it.
2. Ask everyone for one task they do every week that they would happily never do again. Write the list on a wall.
3. Pick the top three. Those are your first three AI sessions, on real work, one person at a time.

You will be in the 2% without spending what the 2% spend, because the training is twenty minutes beside someone, not a course.

Source: Powering Productivity, AI and the Future of UK Work, British Chambers of Commerce, 18 March 2026. https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/news/2026/03/half-of-smes-using-ai-with-limited-headcount-impact-so-far/
