# Held: the audit post

Written 21 September 2026, not scheduled. Week one stays as it is so we can read the numbers
before changing anything. This is the first candidate for week three, or a straight replacement
for the permission post if that one underperforms.

**Asset** `out/find-your-four.png` · **Format** numbered list card, `list.mjs`
**Mechanism** the action line per item. The highest-performing single image in the reference
library (482 reactions, 178 comments) is five numbered steps where each one ends in the exact
thing to do, because the reader leaves with a step rather than a principle.
**Intention** the reader finishes with a written list that is theirs. Where the permission post
takes the pressure off and stops, this one hands over a method that gets better every month.

> Your four are already in your calendar.
>
> Everybody has about four things they actually use these tools for. The trouble is that almost nobody can tell you what their four are, so they keep shopping instead of getting better at the ones they have.
>
> Here is the twenty minutes that fixes that. Do it once, on last week.
>
> **Start from your own week, not a list of use cases.** Open last week's calendar and your sent items and nothing else. List every task over twenty minutes that produced a document or a decision. Most people get fifteen or twenty rows.
>
> **Cross off anything with no file behind it.** If the input only ever existed in your head, it is not ready to hand over. Keep the rows where you could name the file you would attach. This usually halves the list, and the half that survives is the half worth working on.
>
> **Circle what you did more than once.** Frequency beats difficulty every time. A ten-minute job you do daily is worth more than an afternoon you spend twice a year, and it is the one you will actually build the habit on.
>
> **Write each survivor as a prompt, once.** Name who is reading it. Say what to do where the file runs out. Say what to attach. Three lines, not three paragraphs. Keep them in one note, titled by the job rather than by the tool, so the note survives you changing tools.
>
> **Review it in a month and delete what you never opened.** This is the step everybody skips and it is the one that compounds. A list that gets shorter and sharper beats a list that grows. Only add a fifth when it genuinely beats something already on it.
>
> What you have at the end is small and unglamorous: one note, four prompts, written in your own words. But it is the first thing about your job that has ever been written down properly, and the next person who does your role can pick it up on day one.
>
> That is the whole game. Not more tools. A shorter list you keep sharpening.
>
