# The formats, and the evidence for each

Written 21 September 2026 from `references/` — the winners library (posts ranked by the multiple
of their own author's median), the hundred cross-industry posts LinkedIn curated, the
310,000-post hook study, and the sheets Viren has sent in.

This is the reference for what to build and when. `plan.md` says how the week runs; this says
what a format is for and what makes each one fail.

---

## What holds across all of them

**The image carries the post, the words carry the reader in.** In the hundred curated posts, 62
were single image, 36 text, 2 multi-image. Neither wins outright. What wins is when the two do
different jobs: the image is the artefact, the body is the argument for reading it.

**The hook is under forty characters and it is a claim.** 0 to 40 characters earns 2.61% median
engagement, 200 or more earns 2.08%. A story opener is the strongest style at 2.60%, then
contrarian at 2.31%. The question opener is last at 2.16%, and a closing question moves the
median by 0.07 of a point, which is nothing.

**Every item gives the words, not the advice.** The highest-performing single image in the whole
reference set (482 reactions, 178 comments) is five numbered items where each one ends with the
exact sentence to say. That is the difference between a reader agreeing and a reader using it.

**The byline is not compulsory.** A sheet with one clear message is stronger without a name bar
under it. Put the name and the link on the ones a stranger might screenshot out of context: the
grid, the comparison sheet, the last slide of a carousel. Leave it off single-idea cards. Every
renderer now omits the footer when nothing is set.

**Nothing on the canvas that is not doing a job.** Two references lost a full point of
cleanliness for decoration: an iceberg illustration in the middle of a grid, and a hand-drawn
circle that fought a phone screen.

## 1. The grid

`grid.mjs` · one image · 1080x1350

One topic covered to exhaustion. Twenty-five cards, five columns, a family colour, each card a
title and one line. Heavy title bar, quiet footer.

**Evidence.** The grouped library is the highest-multiple repeatable format in the winners set
(Hills 4.3x his own median). The fifty-use-case sheet scores 5 for virality, shareability and
saveability. It is the format that gets kept.

**Use it for** a map of something the reader half knows: every prompt for a role, every question
to ask before signing, every setting worth changing.

**It fails when** the topic is not actually exhaustible, so the sheet has padding in it, or when
the colours group nothing and the eye has to work. Six colours maximum, and each one means
something.

**Cadence** once a fortnight. Each one has to be genuinely complete or it is not this format.

## 2. The comparison sheet

`versus.mjs` · one image · 1080x1350

One pair, held side by side, six times. Common version left in grey, the version that works
right in ink, a `vs` badge on the rule between.

**Evidence.** The culture sheet carries 75 reposts against 83 comments, the highest
repost-to-reaction ratio anything in the library. That ratio is the signal: forwarding a
comparison makes a point for the sender, which is a stronger reason to share than agreement.

**Use it for** a distinction the reader is getting wrong in a way that costs them. Both columns
have to be fair. A straw man on the left kills it.

**It fails when** the left column is a caricature nobody actually types, or when the right column
is a principle rather than a line they can paste.

**Cadence** once a fortnight, alternating with the grid.

## 3. The nested layers diagram

Not built. `layers.mjs` when we need it.

Concentric rounded panels, outermost the broadest, working inward to the daily action. Six
layers, three or six bullets a layer, a colour per ring.

**Evidence.** 762 reactions, 77 reposts on a single image with no photograph and no personality
in it at all. The shape does the work: a reader can see the whole system and their own position
in it at once.

**Use it for** something with a genuine outside-in order, where the inner ring is what you do on
Monday morning.

**It fails when** the rings are just categories in a circle, and when the innermost ring is the
product. On the reference, the centre panel is the author's own tool and the bottom bar is his
domain. That is the whole post pointing inward at a purchase, and it is exactly what we do not
do. Ours would put the reader's own next action in the centre and the link in the body text.

## 3b. The numbered list card

`list.mjs` · one image · 1080x1350

Five numbered steps, each a bold title, a line of explanation and the exact thing to do, on a
green chip that reads Do this.

**Evidence.** The single highest-performing image in the whole reference library: 482 reactions,
178 comments, 44 reposts. The action line is why. The reader leaves with a sentence in their
mouth rather than a principle in their head.

**Use it for** a method someone can run this week without asking a question.

**It fails when** an item ends in advice. If you cannot write the action line, the item is not
finished.

## 4. The prompt card

`render.mjs`, template `prompt` · one image

One real prompt, large enough to read off a phone, deep green, with what to attach underneath.

**Evidence.** The cheapest post to make well, and the one that most reliably gets saved rather
than liked. The single-statement card with nothing on it but the words is confirmed by the
tweet-card references at every scale.

**Use it for** the Tuesday and Thursday slots. One prompt, given completely, no commentary.

**It fails when** the prompt is shortened to fit. If it does not fit, the font comes down or the
prompt is not ready.

## 5. The voice card

`render.mjs`, template `tweet` · one image

Near-black, his face, the verified tick, two or three lines.

**Evidence.** Four parallel lines on a white ground took 3,828 reactions. But that reach is the
author's audience, not the format. Copied without the audience it buys nothing, which is why
this is the rarest card we make and it has to carry a real position.

**Use it for** a belief he will defend in the comments. Never for a fact, never for a tip.

**It fails when** it is a platitude. The test: could anyone in the industry have posted it. If
yes, it is not a voice card.

## 6. The text carousel

`carousel.mjs`, types `cover` / `prompts` / `note` / `close` · PDF document post

Deep green bookends, stone body slides, one idea a slide, prompts quoted in full.

**Evidence.** Document posts hold the reader inside the feed, and the swipe is the cheapest
engagement signal on the platform. The five-page reference took 1,313 reactions on one statement
a slide with nothing under it.

**Use it for** a method with an order to it, where slide three only makes sense after slide two.

**It fails when** it is a list that did not need an order, or when slides are crowded. One idea a
slide, at full size.

**Length.** Five to nine slides for a method. The twenty-four-page reference works because each
page is one rule with three short lines under it and the reader can stop anywhere. Long is
allowed if every page stands alone; long is fatal if the value is back-loaded.

## 7. The photo carousel

`carousel.mjs`, types `photocover` / `photo` · PDF document post

Full-bleed photographs, a bracketed number, one lowercase line. No graphic anywhere.

**Evidence.** 460 reactions and 253 comments on nine photographs with almost no design in them.
The comment count against the reaction count is the tell: photographs of a real person pull
replies that a designed sheet does not. The twenty-four-page writing carousel does the same
thing with a photograph cover and coloured text slides after it, which is the hybrid worth
stealing.

**Use it for** the habits, positions and small rules Viren actually works by. This is the only
format on the board that reads as a person rather than a publisher.

**It fails when** the photographs are stock. There is no version of this that survives a stock
photograph, so the renderer draws a dashed panel naming the file it wants rather than filling
the gap.

**The hybrid.** Photograph cover, then designed slides. Best of both: the cover earns the stop
because it is a face, the slides carry the value because they are legible. Supported today by
mixing `photocover` with the existing body slides.

## Choosing, in one line each

| If the thing you have is | Build |
|---|---|
| a complete map of a subject | the grid |
| a method someone runs once, in order | the numbered list card |
| a distinction people get wrong | the comparison sheet |
| an order of operations, outside in | the layers diagram, once built |
| one prompt worth the whole post | the prompt card |
| a position you will defend | the voice card |
| a method where step three needs step two | the text carousel |
| something true about how you work | the photo carousel |

## The cover rules, for any carousel

The cover promises one specific thing and the deck delivers all of it. A number in the title
sets the contract: twenty-two rules means twenty-two slides. The cover is the only slide allowed
a headline that is not itself useful. Slide two has to pay immediately, because that is where
the reader decides.

## Before anything ships

Scored on the five in `../references/scoring.md`. Then: does it sell anything (it must not), is
every word plain, is the hook under forty characters and complete above the fold, is there a
question close (there must not be), and does every numbered item give the words rather than the
advice.
