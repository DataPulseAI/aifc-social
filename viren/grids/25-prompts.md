# Grid 01: 25 prompts your team will actually use

Image: `out/25-prompts.png`, 1080x1350 at 2x. Single image post.

## The post

Nobody needs another list of AI tips.

Here are 25 prompts instead. One for each job that quietly eats a Tuesday, written in plain words, for whichever assistant your company already pays for.

A few that people keep coming back to:

The tender one pulls every requirement into a table in the buyer's own words, including the one buried on page 38.

The spreadsheet one explains a sheet somebody else built: what is an input, what is calculated, and every place it quietly does not add up.

The complaint one writes a reply that owns the problem and commits to something, without grovelling and without deflecting.

The board paper one plays a sceptical non-executive and gives you the eight questions they will ask, in the order they will ask them.

The image is the whole map. The page has the full text of each one, what to attach, and what good output looks like, because a prompt without those two things is a party trick.

## Why it is built this way

Hook is a contrarian line of 36 characters and it stands alone above the fold. Contrarian
openers sit second of five in the 310k-post study (`references/authoredup/`) and the fold is
where the whole decision happens.

Four prompts are named in the post, not all 25, so the image has a job. Each named one is a
moment a reader recognises, not a category.

The link is in the post, not the comments. The page has no form on it, which is the reason the
line "no email, no form" is worth saying and is the reason it will be believed.

Nothing here mentions a service, a day, a session or an offer. Someone who wants that clicks
the profile, and the profile does that work.

## Scored against our own reference sheet

to a team without adding a caveat. Applicability 5, every prompt is already written and live.

## Replies to have ready

"Which tool is this for." Whichever you already pay for. Each one on the page names the tool it
was written for and most work anywhere.

"Do they work with our data." Every one of them is written so it does not need personal data.
Where a task touches people, the prompt asks for roles and situations, not names.

"Can I have them as a PDF." They are a web page so they stay current. Save the image.
