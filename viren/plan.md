# Viren's profile: the content plan

21 September 2026. Four weeks, three posts a week, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, between 7:30
and 8:30. Every post below is built from something real: your events, your product, your
service, your last month of posts. Nothing needs a former employer or a technical word.

## What the strong accounts actually do (the whole lesson, short)

Lara Acosta (median 1,641 reactions): first person, a face or a place in every photo, one
moment per post, 20 short lines, a warm plain close. Her two best posts ever are hiring posts.
Heather Murray (UK, our closest competitor, £1m business): her top ten is an award, a joke, a
dog, a team photo, a confession. Her AI content is a minority. The AuthoredUp examples
(updated 28 August 2026): a bold plain claim, a personal story with a number, a misconception
named and corrected, all in words anyone understands. Your own feed: the Convo #01 story did
58 and carried a quoted line ("Nobody's ever actually asked me that"); the product pitch pasted
into five groups did 4 to 9 each.

So the rules are five, in `brand.json`. Plain English. One idea with a real moment up front.
A photo of a real room or person. Under 150 words unless a story earns more. No jargon, no
question close, ask only for something real.

## Hooks

Every hook above is under forty characters and stands alone before "see more", which the
AuthoredUp hook study (updated 11 August 2026) found earns the most from a scanning reader. Two
shapes only: a story dropped mid-scene with a number ("Forty strangers. One Soho pub."), or a
flat statement someone could argue with ("Most AI training is a demo."). No questions as hooks;
a yes-or-no question hands the reader an exit.

## The mix

Each week: one personal (a room, a moment, a person), one useful (something the reader can do
this week, said plainly, sometimes a carousel), one proof (a real screen, a real number, a real
message, showing the thing is happening). AI for Companies appears in the useful and proof
slots as belief and evidence, never as a pitch. Convu appears as the rooms you run and the
people in them.

## How it runs each week

Sunday: I draft the three posts for the week from this plan and what you have given me, and put
them in your Buffer approval queue as text with the photo slot marked. Monday: you read them on
your phone, change a line, add the photo, approve or bin. Each morning: post goes out, you reply
to comments for fifteen minutes. Thursday evening or Sunday: you send me the three things for
next week (a photo, a line someone said, a number). That is under an hour of your week.

Carousels are built with the Convu carousel skill in your palette, big type, one line a slide.
Photos are yours; nothing is stock. If a week has no photo, the personal slot becomes text and
loses nothing.
