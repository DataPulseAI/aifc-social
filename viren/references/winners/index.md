reaction post from a 300k follower.

Every row was read on the day. Where the post is openable, the id goes to
`linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:<id>`.

## What the multiples say, in order

**1. The grouped library with a number is the most repeatable high performer.** Hills 4.3x and
3.0x, Hassid 3.5x and 3.2x. Reposts run 100 to 300 on these, which is the only mechanism that
reaches people who do not follow you. It needs a real collection and a real number.
*Ours:* the 25-prompt grid. Built.

**2. The original count is the cheapest 4x on the board.** Grandillon got 4.2x from a 69-median
account by counting 49 tools himself. Banks got 3.1x mapping humanoid makers. Nobody else has
the number, so nobody else can post it.
*Ours: not built yet. Highest-value gap.*

**3. Permission outperforms instruction.** Hassid 2.5x for "stop doom-scrolling, pick one tool",
1.9x for "you can't keep up, me neither". Donnelly 5.8x and 4.9x for two permission posts.
Heather 1.5x for admitting she has never used Claude Code, as an AI trainer.
*Ours: one a fortnight, and it has to be true.*

**4. A real person in a real place beats everything a company can post.** Tobi's brother, Lara's
childhood room, Viren's own Convo #01 at 6.8x, his own highest multiple in the set.
*Ours: Wednesday, whenever a photograph exists.*

**5. Hiring posts are disproportionate.** Lara's two best ever, Viren's second best, Rowan
Cheung's best of the year, and Cheung's was text only.

**6. Short is not punished.** Heather's 2.4x was 114 characters. Isenberg's 1.6x was 565
characters of plain text with no image and no ask. Length is not what earns the read.

## The two accounts Viren named, read honestly

**Adam Biddlecombe.** Median 165 from 45 posts, every one of his top nine an image post of about
2,550 characters, and every one of the top nine opens "Most people" or "Most founders". The body
is what is worth taking, and it is excellent: contrarian claim, the cost of the belief in three
short paragraphs, a one-sentence reframe ("Just a loop: understand, plan, act, check, repeat"),
then the complete ordered list, twelve items, each a name and one or two lines, then a two-line
summary of the whole stack, then reassurance. That structure is the "well packed, one topic"
thing Viren likes.
**Refuse:** the hook family (it is the single most replicated opener in AI LinkedIn and it is
banned in our voice rules), "I got this wrong for years", and the four stacked asks at the end,
one of which is literally "Genuinely curious".

**Heather Murray.** Median 108 from 46 posts. She runs a £1m AI training business and **her top
ten contains no pitch at all**: an award, a joke, the £1m milestone, a paintball day, three
things about working from home, a team photo, an IBM visit, an ode to her co-founder, admitting
she has never used Claude Code, and how she built the business. The training is the thing she
sells and it is almost never the thing she posts.
*This is the cadence model. The company appears as a fact about her life, roughly once a week,
and never as an offer.*

## What we will not copy, and why

The "Most people" opener, the one-sentence-per-line stack, comment-to-get-the-resource
(Donnelly's 3,090-comment posts, banned in `docs/01` and downranked by LinkedIn since March
2026), the question close, and the four stacked asks. Every one of them works for an account
with 300k followers absorbing the cost. At 3,693 followers we cannot absorb it, and the reader
now has a "seems like AI slop" button.
