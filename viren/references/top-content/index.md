# LinkedIn's own top content, ten industries, captured 21 September 2026

Source: https://www.linkedin.com/top-content/ , the hubs LinkedIn curates itself. One hundred
posts across marketing, sales, leadership, technology, finance, productivity, recruitment and
HR, customer experience, business strategy and workplace trends. Full data in `posts.json`
(author, headline, followers, age, hook, body, character count, format, image, permalink).
Fourteen images saved in `img/`, contact sheet at `contact.jpg`.

Deliberately not an AI list. The point was to see what travels in industries with nothing to
do with ours.

## What the hundred say

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Format | 62 image, 36 text, 2 multi-image |
| Hook style | 72 statement, 12 story, 8 results, 6 contrarian, 2 question |
| Body length | median 1,881 characters, range 355 to 2,998 |
| Hook length | median 67 characters |
| Smallest account curated | 7,513 followers |

Four things worth changing our behaviour over.

**Text is a third of it.** Thirty-six of a hundred posts LinkedIn itself picked carry no image.
We have been treating the image as compulsory. It is not, and a text post costs nothing to make.

**The flat statement dominates.** Seventy-two of a hundred. Story is the highest-scoring opener
in the AuthoredUp data but it is the rarest in what actually gets curated, because most people
have no story on most days. The statement is the workhorse and it is what we should default to.

**Length is real.** Median 1,881 characters. Our posts have been running 1,300 to 1,500. We are
short of the middle of the distribution, and Donnelly's Q3 read says the same. Write longer.

**Audience is not the entry fee.** A 7,513-follower account and a 10,080-follower account both
made the cut. Both did it with a specific, checkable thing, not with reach.

## The three image archetypes that repeat across every industry

**1. The dense framework grid** (`img/tc-25.jpg`, 82,307 followers, leadership). Seven numbered
panels on one sheet, each a named framework with a micro-diagram, under one banner headline.
Everything about the topic, in one image, colour-coded.
Virality 4, shareability 5, applicability 5, cleanliness 3, saveability 5.
*This is the format we built as `grid.mjs`. Seeing it in leadership, not AI, confirms it
transfers. Marked down on cleanliness because seven palettes fight each other.*

**2. The numbered list card** (`img/tc-62.jpg`, 39,054 followers, recruitment). Cream ground, a
two-line headline, ten numbered items, each a bold three-word lead-in then one or two plain
sentences. No illustration at all.
Virality 4, shareability 5, applicability 5, cleanliness 5, saveability 5.
*The highest combined score in the set, from a small account, with no design budget. The bold
lead-in per item is the detail we have not been using and should.*

**3. The annotated teardown** (`img/tc-81.jpg`, 936,564 followers, business strategy, and
`img/tc-8.jpg`, 39,133 followers, marketing). A real artefact shown whole, with a labelled
column down the side naming each part, or a before and after side by side with numbered
callouts on the differences.
Virality 4, shareability 5, applicability 5, cleanliness 4, saveability 5.
*The before-and-after version is the "old versus new" Viren asked for, and the 39k account
proves it does not need a famous name. We have the artefacts to do this: a prompt, annotated.*

## What this changes for us

1. Write longer. Target 1,800 to 2,400 characters, not 1,400.
2. Default to the statement hook. Keep story for the days there is a real one.
3. Run text-only posts without guilt; a third of curated top content has no image.
4. Add the bold lead-in per item to list cards and grid cards.
5. Build the annotated teardown, because it is the "old versus new" format and we have the
   artefacts for it.
6. Find our version of the "In Case of Death folder": one piece of admin everyone knows they
   should do, given away complete.
