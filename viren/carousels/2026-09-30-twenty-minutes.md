## The post that goes with it

84% of UK workers had no AI training last year.

Not too little. None. That is the government's own survey of people in work, published in January, and it sits next to two other numbers: 21% feel confident using AI at work, and 21% say it has made them more productive.

Every company I walk into has already bought the licences. An email went round. A few people worked it out on their own, and everyone else quietly went back to the old way, because nobody showed them and asking felt like admitting something.

You do not need a budget to fix that. You need twenty minutes, one person, and a file they already hate.

The whole method is in the carousel. Eight steps, nothing to buy, and the only one people skip is step three.

The short version: pick one person, not the team. Pick the task they dread, not an impressive one. Open their real file. Type one plain sentence, the kind you would say to a good new starter. Read the answer out loud together before anyone copies anything. Say what is wrong and ask again, because the second prompt is where the skill lives. Save the sentence somewhere they will find it on Monday.

Then next week they run it for someone else, and you are out of the loop.

Source: AI Skills for Life and Work, general public survey findings, DCMS and DSIT, 28 January 2026, 591 people in work. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-skills-for-life-and-work-general-public-survey-findings/ai-skills-for-life-and-work-general-public-survey-findings

## Why this is built the way it is

Hook is a statement of 46 characters and stands alone above the fold; statement openers sit at
2.27% median and question openers last, per the 310k-post study in `../references/authoredup/`.
No closing question: it is worth 0.07 of a percentage point and reads as bait. The link is in
the post, not the comments, which is the one thing Donnelly's Q3 read is unambiguous about.

The carousel is the save. The post is the share. The method is given away complete, so nobody
has to comment for it, which is the mechanism we refuse.

## Reply plan for the first hour

Have two replies ready.

If someone says their team is too busy: "Twenty minutes on a task they already do, not twenty
minutes extra. The first one usually pays for itself the same week."

If someone asks which tool: "Whichever one you already pay for. The lesson is identical in
Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, and switching tools is the most common way to avoid
starting."

If someone asks for the slides as a PDF: they are the post, tell them to save it.
