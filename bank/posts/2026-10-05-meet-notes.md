Google Meet started taking minutes on 29 September, and somebody at your firm still needs to decide whether that is on by default.

The Workspace update is narrow. End users can enable "Take notes for me" for meetings with three or more participants, and the settings took effect on 29 September 2026. That is the whole change.

The decision it forces is not narrow. An automatic note taker changes what people say in a meeting, and it changes it most for the meetings where candour was the point: a performance conversation, a supplier dispute, an early conversation about redundancies or a deal.

Three things worth settling now, if they were not settled last week:

Who can switch it on. If that is everyone, say so, rather than discovering it in a meeting where it matters.

What happens to the notes. They are a record. Treat them like one, which means knowing where they live and who can read them later.

Whether anyone says it out loud. Telling a room it is being minuted by software costs five seconds and prevents the only version of this that becomes a problem.

None of that requires a policy document. It requires one person to have made the call, and the room to know what the call was.

This is not legal advice.
