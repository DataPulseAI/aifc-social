Security, no clear use, and accuracy. Those are the three things UK small business owners name when asked what stops them using AI, and each one has a first move that takes under an hour.

The figures are from Simply Business, published 8 September 2026, from 1,842 small business owners surveyed between 30 July and 7 August. Security and privacy was named by 44%, not seeing a clear use for AI by 39%, and concerns about accuracy by 36%.

What is worth noticing is that only the first of those is about the technology. The second is about your own work, and the third is about how you check things. Neither gets solved by a better tool.

The first moves are on the card. The one that changes the most is the second: pick a task from last week that took too long and was not interesting, and try it on that. Not a demonstration task, and not a task you have already made easy. People decide whether AI is for them on the first thing they try, and most firms let that first thing be an email that was fine anyway.

On accuracy, the useful discipline is narrow. Anything with a number, a name or a date gets checked against the source. Everything else does not need checking, because a rewritten paragraph that is slightly off is a paragraph you rewrite.
