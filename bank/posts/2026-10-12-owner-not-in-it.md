Whoever owns your AI rollout should not be in IT.

IT should buy it, secure it, and tell you where the data goes. Those are real jobs and nobody else in the building can do them. But the moment the seats are live, the work that decides whether this was worth paying for is not technical, and handing it to the people who handled procurement is how a rollout becomes a helpdesk queue.

The questions that actually come up in week three are these. Is this draft good enough to send to a client? Does our tone survive it? Which parts of this process are we now allowed to skip, and which are we not? What do I do when it is confidently wrong about something I half know?

Nobody in IT can answer those, and it is unfair to ask. They are questions about your trade, and they can only be settled by someone who does the work and whose judgement the team already accepts. Usually that is a senior person in the function, not the most enthusiastic person in the function, and those are rarely the same individual.

What we would do instead is name an owner per function rather than one for the company. The person who owns it in finance is not the person who owns it in sales, because a good output looks different in each. Give each of them one hour a week, in the diary, for the first six weeks, and one explicit job: decide what good looks like here and write it down where the team can see it.

That is a small amount of time and it is the whole difference. A tool with no owner gets used by the three people who were going to use it anyway. A tool with an owner who does the work gets used by the people who were waiting to be told it was safe to.
