Six jobs to hand your assistant this week. None of them take longer than ten minutes and all of them are work you were going to do anyway.

1. The policy nobody reads. Paste it in and ask for the same rules at reading age twelve, on one page. Then read both and see which one you would actually circulate.

2. Meeting notes into actions. Paste the notes, ask for owners and dates only, nothing else. The "nothing else" is the instruction that makes it useful.

3. The email you are avoiding. Describe the situation and the outcome you want, ask for three versions. Send none of them as written. You will have the shape of it in two minutes instead of forty.

4. Two lists that should match. Give it both exports and ask what is in one and not the other. This is the one that surprises people, because it is the job nobody thinks to give it.

5. Prep for a hard call. Ask it what the client will object to, before they do. It will get about half of them, and the half it gets are the ones you had not thought about.

6. The job advert. Describe the person you want rather than the role, and let it write the role. The adverts that work describe a person.

The pattern underneath all six: you are not asking it to know something, you are asking it to do something to material you already have. That is where the time goes, and it is the use almost nobody arrives at on their own.

Pick one. Not six.
