You don't need an AI strategy. You need one person who tries it on Tuesday.

A strategy is what big companies write when they have a team to write it. In a firm of 15 or 50 it usually goes like this: someone says "we should look at AI", a meeting gets booked, and three months later nobody has typed a word into it.

What moves things is smaller and a bit boring. One person, one job they do every week, and the AI you already have. ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini, whichever it is, even the free version.

Our read is that the person matters more than the tool. Pick someone who knows the job inside out, not whoever is "good with computers". They'll know in seconds whether what comes back is any use.

Here's what we'd do. On Monday, ask them to choose the weekly job they least like: the supplier chase, the meeting notes, the price list that needs tidying. On Tuesday they try it with AI, once. On Friday they tell you in two minutes what happened, good or bad.

If it helped, they do it again next week. If it didn't, they pick another job. After a month you'll know more than a strategy document would have told you.
