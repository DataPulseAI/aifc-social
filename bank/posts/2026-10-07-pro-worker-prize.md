The UK government is running a prize for AI that creates jobs rather than cutting them. Nominations close at 11:55pm on 7 October.

It is DSIT's Pro-Worker AI Adoption Prize, and the judging panel is chaired by the Nobel Prize-winning economist Simon Johnson. Any UK business, charity or public sector organisation with at least five UK staff can be nominated, and nominations are open to businesses, workers, trade unions and investors. The top 50 are shortlisted. Three winners get a detailed case study written by UK business school professors, funded by grants from the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation.

Read the five criteria even if you never nominate anyone, because they are a better test of an AI project than most business cases we see:

1. It increases productivity.
2. It gives workers new capabilities.
3. It creates new roles or jobs.
4. It creates new products or services that increase demand for human expertise.
5. It puts worker voices at the centre of how AI is adopted.

Run your own AI project against those five. Our read is that most pass the first and fail the other four, and that the fourth is the one almost nobody has considered at all.

If you clear three of the five, you have a nomination worth making and two days to make it.
