The prompts that survive are never the impressive ones.

We publish 25. The three that get opened most are, in order: turn these notes into decisions with owners and dates, tell me what is in one of these lists and not the other, and tell me what this client will object to before they do.

None of those is clever. What they have in common is that each one is attached to a moment. The Monday after the meeting. The month end when two systems disagree. The hour before a difficult call.

The impressive prompts, the long ones with roles and personas and step by step reasoning, get tried once in a training session and never again. Not because they do not work. Because nobody can remember when to use them.

So the useful exercise is not collecting prompts. It is writing down the five moments in your team's week where somebody is doing something dull with material that already exists, and then writing one prompt for each. Five is enough. Most teams that do this end up using two of the five heavily and quietly dropping the rest, which is the correct outcome.
