Cost is the barrier small firms name most often. It is not the one that stops them.

Enterprise Nation asked 1,320 UK micro and small businesses what gets in the way of using AI, with Google, Sage, Dell and Square, published 9 June. The answers, in order:

Cost of software and setting it up, 53%. Lack of skills, 46%. Data protection or privacy, 38%. No time to learn and set up the tools, 37%. Cyber security risk, 33%. Uncertainty about whether it pays off, 32%. No trusted advice, 30%.

Read the list again and notice how few of those are actually about money.

A ChatGPT or Copilot seat is roughly the price of a couple of coffees a week. That is not what 53% are describing. They are describing the setting up: the hours of working out what it is for, which of your processes it touches, what you are allowed to put in it, and who in the building is going to show everyone else. Nobody sells that and nobody budgets for it, so it turns up in the survey as cost.

The same thing is happening one row down. "Lack of skills" reads like a hiring problem. In a twelve-person company it almost never is. It is that nobody has sat with the finance manager and shown her, on her own reconciliation, what the thing actually does.

And 30% say they have no trusted advice. That is the quietest number on the list and the cheapest to fix.

Source: Enterprise Nation Tech Hub, Digital and AI adoption among UK SMEs, 9 June 2026, n=1,320.
