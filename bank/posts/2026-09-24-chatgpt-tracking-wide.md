Free ChatGPT accounts are being tracked across about a thousand other websites, according to one researcher.

The finding is from a threat intelligence researcher writing as Buchodi, published on 20 September and read by us this morning. OpenAI's ad collector at bzr.openai.com sets a cookie called __obi on chatgpt.com, and OpenAI pixels on advertiser sites read the same identifier back. The researcher counted 936 advertiser pixels across 1,029 hostnames and tested twelve retail and education sites to watch the cookie travel. OpenAI's own cookie policy, updated 10 September, lists __obi under Analytics rather than marketing, so refusing marketing cookies does not switch it off.

Two things to hold at once. This is one researcher's analysis. Others have repeated it, nobody has independently reproduced it that we can find, and OpenAI acknowledged the researcher's questions on 14 September and has not answered them. Treat it as an allegation with evidence attached, not a finding.

And: adverts appear on the free tiers. Paid plans do not carry them. So the account most likely to be tracked is the free personal one, which is also the account most likely to be quietly used for work at a small firm, because the firm never bought seats.

That is the part for you. The line between a personal account and a company one was always a data question. This week it also looks like an advertising question. If anyone on your team pastes client material into a free account, the question is not whether the assistant is good enough. It is whose account it is.
