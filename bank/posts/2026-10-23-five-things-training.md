A day of AI training has five jobs to do. Here are all five.

Ask them first. Survey the people coming: what they actually do all week, and what they already use. A session built on guesses teaches the wrong half of the room.

Build it on their real tasks, in the tool they already have. Not a demo account, not something nobody will have on Monday. Getting it to transfer is the whole point.

Agree the guardrails in the room. What never gets pasted in, decided while everyone is there to argue about it, sticks better than a policy circulated afterwards.

Leave a library the team owns. The prompts that worked, written down, with one named person responsible for keeping it.

Come back at week four. Three numbers: minutes saved per person per week, how many of the saved prompts are still in use, and how many people used the tool that week.

That is the whole shape of it. If you are buying training from anyone, including us, those are the five things to ask whether their day actually does.
