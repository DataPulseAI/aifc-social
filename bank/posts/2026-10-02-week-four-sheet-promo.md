Ten minutes, three questions, and you will know whether the AI you bought is being used.

Most firms find this out by asking the vendor, and the vendor dashboard answers a question nobody asked: how many people have ever logged in. That number only goes up. It was 100% the week after training and it will be 100% in March.

The three questions on the card take a morning coffee. Ask them of last week, not of since launch.

The combinations are where the answer is. Plenty of opens and no named tasks being done with it means the training taught the tool rather than the job, which is the most common outcome and the most fixable. Few opens and a strong answer on time saved means two or three people have quietly worked it out and nobody has asked them how. That second one is the best possible result and the one firms most often read as failure.

The reason to do this at four weeks rather than at six months is that four weeks is early enough for a bad answer to be cheap. At six months the licence has renewed and somebody has an opinion to defend.

One thing not to do: do not ask people whether they find it useful. You will get a satisfaction score, which is a measure of how much they like you. Ask how long one specific task took this time compared with the last time.
