Stop showing your team AI demos.

Nobody has learned to use a tool by watching someone else use it. The demo is the most common shape of AI training in UK firms right now, and it is the reason a licence gets opened twice and left alone. Someone confident types a clever prompt, the room says that is good, and by Tuesday nobody can remember what was typed or why it worked on that example and not on theirs.

A demo shows the tool at its best on a task the presenter chose. The team's work is not that task. It is the tender that closes Thursday, the supplier reconciliation nobody wants, the board pack that takes a day and a half. Until the assistant has been pointed at one of those, by the person who owns it, with their own file attached, nothing has been learned. Something has been watched.

The tell is the question people ask afterwards. After a demo it is "what can it do?". After an hour on their own work it is "why did it get that wrong?", which is the question you want, because the answer is where the skill lives.

What we would do instead. Cancel the next demo. Ask each person to bring the one task on their week that takes longest and is least interesting, and give them an hour with the assistant the firm already pays for. Then ask what broke. That conversation is the training.
