A prompt is not a clever sentence. It is a job description, and most of them fail for the same reason a bad job advert fails.

Here is the shape that works, in four parts, and it takes about five minutes to write one.

Name the material. "Here is the tender document and here is our draft." Attach both. Almost everything useful an assistant does is done to something you already have, and the single biggest difference between people who get value and people who do not is whether they attach the file.

Name the output, including what it must not contain. "Owners and dates only, nothing else." The restriction does more work than the instruction. Without it you get a summary with your actions buried in the middle of it.

Name the standard. "List every question in the document we have not answered." That is checkable. "Make it better" is not, and an assistant asked to make something better will make it longer.

Then use it again next week. A prompt that gets written once and never reused was a demonstration. The ones that survive are the ones attached to a moment in the week: the Monday report, the tender, the board pack.

One test before you save it. Read the prompt and ask whether a new starter could follow it without asking you a question. If not, the assistant cannot either.
