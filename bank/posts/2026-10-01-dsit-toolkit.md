DSIT published an AI risk toolkit on 8 September, and it is a spreadsheet rather than a lecture.

It is written for anyone designing, operating, procuring or delivering a product that uses AI, which includes the person at a 40-person firm deciding whether to switch on a feature their supplier is offering. The download is guidance plus two workbooks, one Excel and one OpenDocument, and the workbooks are the useful part.

Six things in it that are worth an hour of somebody's time this week:

1. It is aimed at multi-disciplinary teams, so it names IT, project delivery, change and communications as owners, not just data science.
2. It makes you write the risk down before you write the mitigation, which is the step most internal AI reviews skip.
3. It separates designing, procuring and delivering. Most small firms only ever procure, and that column is shorter than people fear.
4. It asks you to justify the treatment you chose, not only to record it.
5. The workbook format means it survives contact with a real organisation. A PDF gets read once.
6. It is free and it is the UK government's own framing, which is worth something when a client asks how you assess this.

It will not tell you whether to buy the thing. It will stop the assessment being a conversation that happened once and was never written down.

This is not legal advice.
