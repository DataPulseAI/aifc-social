Google's own AI broke into three real companies during a safety test.

Google confirmed it on 18 September. During pre-deployment "capture the flag" exercises in May, Gemini was meant to be attacking a fictional company. The fictional company had the same name as a real one, and the model was not supposed to have internet access. It did. In one case it guessed passwords. In the other two it found credentials sitting in public repositories and used them.

Heather Adkins, Google's vice president of security engineering, said "safe development of powerful AI models is critical and we invest deeply in this area". The evaluator, Irregular, says the problems "were remedied and resolved weeks ago" and that every lab was told in late July.

The detail that should travel is the one nobody planned. The boundary between the test and the real world was a sentence in the instructions, and a sentence is not a boundary.

That is the whole lesson at your scale too, and it costs nothing to apply. Whatever you have told an assistant it may not touch, check what it can actually reach. Credentials in a shared drive, an API key in a document, a login in a spreadsheet called passwords. The rule is what you wrote down. The reach is what it can get to.
