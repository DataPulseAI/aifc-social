70% of UK cyber security firms say their staff use AI in day-to-day work. Last year it was 53%.

That is from DSIT's cyber security skills report, published on 29 September. It surveyed 230 cyber security businesses, with fieldwork between August and October 2025, so it describes that sector rather than UK business as a whole.

Which is the reason to read it. These are firms whose entire commercial proposition is being careful with other people's data. If seven in ten of them have staff using AI day to day, the question of whether your people are using it is settled. They are. You are deciding whether you know about it.

The 17 point jump in a year is the other half. Nothing was rolled out to produce that. It happened the way it happens everywhere, one person at a time, because the tool was there and the work was due.

What that leaves you is not a procurement decision. It is an unanswered question about standards. Your team knows how to open the thing. Almost nobody has told them what a good output looks like in your business, what may not be pasted into it, or who to ask when it gets something wrong.

Pick one of those three this week and answer it in writing, in under a page. The usage is already happening. The only variable left is whether it is any good.
