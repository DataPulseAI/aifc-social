Skills England put three AI tools for employers on gov.uk in June. They are free, and they are barely mentioned anywhere.

They are an AI Skills Framework, which sets out the technical, responsible and non-technical skills by job role; an AI Skills Adoption Pathway Model, which describes the stages an organisation moves through; and an Employer AI Adoption Checklist, which is a set of prompts for working out where your own gaps are.

The work was done with Dr Nisreen Ameen of Royal Holloway, University of London, and drew on more than 150 employers. The same announcement puts daily AI use at around 44% of workplaces, and sets out six principles for training that sticks: practical, reachable, integrated, modular, expandable and sustainable.

Phil Smith, chair of Skills England, said in it that "AI is already transforming how businesses operate but too many are still struggling to unlock its full potential."

The checklist is the one to start with. It is written as a set of prompts for someone who is not technical, and it is aimed at working out where the gaps in your own workforce are rather than at scoring you.

None of this replaces deciding what you actually want people to do differently. It does stop you paying somebody to run a discovery workshop that arrives at the same place.
