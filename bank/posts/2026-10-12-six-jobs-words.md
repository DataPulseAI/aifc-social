Six jobs you'd do this week anyway, and the exact words to type for each.

They work in ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini, whichever you have. None needs anything you don't already have on your desk.

1. A policy nobody reads. Paste it in: "Rewrite this for staff reading on a phone. One page, plain words, keep every rule."

2. Notes from a meeting. Paste them: "List every action as who, what, by when. Write 'no owner' where nobody's named."

3. An email you're putting off. Type: "I need to tell [who] that [what]. Write it three ways: short, warm, firm."

4. Two lists that should match. Paste both: "Show what's in the first list but not the second, then the other way round."

5. Before a hard call. Type: "I'm calling [who] about [what]. What will they push back on? One-line answer to each."

6. A job advert. Type: "Write an advert for [role]. We want someone who [three things]. Under 200 words, no jargon."

What they have in common: you're not asking it to know anything. You're asking it to do something to material you already have, which is where it's most reliable and where most of the time goes.

Two checks before you use the answers. Read the rewritten policy against the original, because "keep every rule" is an instruction it can still miss. And read the advert for anything that hints at age or gender before it goes live.

This is not legal advice.
