Your AI pilot needs an end date, not a bigger budget.

Most of the stalled rollouts we hear about are not failing. They are running. Ten seats, a keen handful, a shared folder of prompts, a line item small enough that nobody has to defend it at the quarterly review. It has been going since spring and it will still be going at Christmas, because a pilot with no decision date cannot fail and therefore cannot finish.

This is comfortable and it is expensive in a way the invoice does not show. The cost is not the licences. It is that your company now has a permanent answer to the question of what it is doing about AI, and that answer is "we are piloting it", which stops anyone asking the harder question for another six months.

A pilot is a question with a date attached. Without the date it is a subscription.

So pick the date before you pick anything else. Eight weeks is usually enough, twelve is generous. Write down now, while nobody is invested, what you will look at on that day and what result would make you stop. Our read is that most teams cannot answer the second one, and that is the tell: a pilot you would never cancel was never a pilot.

Then actually hold the meeting. Either it goes to everyone and someone owns it properly, or it stops and you keep the money. Both of those are good outcomes. The third option, where it quietly continues at ten seats and nobody mentions it again, is the one that has been chosen by default at a great many companies this year, and it was never chosen on purpose.
