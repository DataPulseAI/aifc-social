Five jobs for the assistant that has just appeared in your Word sidebar.

OpenAI put ChatGPT into Microsoft Word on 17 September, on every plan. It drafts from notes, summarises, rewrites and changes headings and formatting, and it sits beside the document rather than in another tab.

The five on the card are chosen on one test: the material is already in the file, so nothing has to be described, pasted or explained. That is where the time actually goes.

The one most people skip is the third. Take a document you send often, a scope, a proposal, a set of terms, and ask what a reader would find unclear on a first read. Not "improve this". You will get back the two paragraphs everyone has been quietly misreading for two years, and that is worth more than any rewrite.

The one to be careful with is the summary. A summary of a document you wrote is useful. A summary of a document you have not read is a decision you have not made.

For professional services firms this lands on the work that is the product, not the admin around it, which is why the checking rule matters more here than anywhere: anything with a client's name, a date or an amount gets read against the source.
