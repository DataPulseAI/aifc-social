Don't hand AI to the youngest person in the office. Hand it to whoever knows the job best.

It is a familiar pattern. Someone decides the 24-year-old should have a look at ChatGPT, because they are quick with their phone and nobody else wants to. Six weeks later there is a folder of clever experiments and nothing anyone else uses.

Being fast with a new tool and knowing what a good quote, a good chaser or a good handover note looks like are two different things. The second is much harder to teach, and it is the one that decides whether the output is any use. Our read is that the bottleneck is almost never the typing.

What we'd do: pick whoever writes the most quotes or emails in a normal week. Give them one job, the one they repeat every Monday, and four weeks. Let them keep the versions that worked somewhere the rest of the team can see. On the Friday of week four, sit down and read three of the outputs together.

The youngest person in the office will still be the fastest. Put them next to the one who knows the job, and you get both.
