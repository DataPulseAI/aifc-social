An office suite published six conditions it would want met before adding AI. They work rather well as a purchase checklist.

The Document Foundation, which makes LibreOffice, set them out on 3 September 2026 while explaining why there is no generative AI in the default install. Its position is not refusal. "LibreOffice does not reject artificial intelligence out of hand." The conclusion is narrower: "There is no integration that meets all the requirements and that could be deployed."

The six are on the card. What makes them useful outside their context is that each one is a question a vendor can answer yes or no to, in writing, in a sentence. Most AI procurement conversations do not contain six of those.

Two are worth pressing on more than the rest.

Where inference happens decides who holds your documents at the moment they are processed, which is a different question from where they are stored and is answered on a different page of the contract.

And whether the feature is genuinely optional. Not "you can turn it off", but installable, removable, and absent from the interface for the people who do not want it. Anything switched on by default becomes the way your firm works within about a fortnight, whatever the policy says.
